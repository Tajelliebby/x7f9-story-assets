Sasi woke with a hiss. Her hand moved before she did. Fingers finding her collarbone, pressing flat against her sternum.

Nothing.

Her eyes snapped open, fingers scrabbled at her throat, searching for something that wasn’t there. The leather cord. The cracked stone. Gone. Everything gone. She sat up too fast, every part of her protested. Lungs first. Then ribs. Then the rest, all at once, as she tried to push herself upright. She stopped trying.

Of all the things to lose. Of all the things the sea had taken from her, why did it have to be that? That one small piece of her mother. That one tether to the before-times, when she’d been someone’s daughter instead of just crew.

Her eyes burned. Her throat tightened, pain clawing at her from the inside. Then she forced it down. Sasi pressed the heel of her hand against her eyes and breathed. In. Out. Steady. Let’s take stock.

Fingers. Present. Toes. Responding, if grudgingly. Her ribs sent a sharp advisory when she drew too full a breath, but nothing shifted wrong, nothing ground against itself. She had cracked ribs before. These were only bruised, which was practically a gift. Her palms were raw, the skin stripped in places from whatever she had been clinging to in the water. Bleedin’ had stopped, at least. Her hair was a salt-stiffened ruin plastered flat against her cheek and throat.

She did a full count. Miraculously, most of her was accounted for.

Aye, reckon the sea ain’t done with ye yet, girl.

Her eyes rolled up. The sky was just beginning its shift from black to indigo, that thin edge of false dawn. Clouds still moved fast overhead, the tail end of last night’s fury dragging itself northward. The wind still carried teeth.

The outcropping of rock behind her had provided shelter through the worst of the storm. Frozen stone, jagged and unwelcoming, but better than the open beach. She couldn’t quite remember how she’d gotten here. The ship breaking apart. The water filling her lungs. A pull. A hand. A voice that had sounded like the ocean itself.

She must have dreamed most of it.

Sasi rolled onto her side with a groan, pressing herself up on both raw palms, and got her first proper look at where the sea had deposited her. The shore was black. Not dark sand, not storm-grey shale. Genuinely black.

The ocean stretched out before her, tranquil now, its fury spent. The rhythmic lap of water against shore was almost gentle. It was remarkably pretty.

Also remarkably lacking in fresh water.

She licked her lips. Cracked and salty. Her tongue felt like leather. She needed fresh water, needed it badly enough that she forced herself to sit up, despite the knife of pain that lanced through her chest.

“Aye, take it easy now,” she muttered, gritting her teeth. “Protest noted, knees. Keep movin’.”

She shifted her weight to the right and waited for the agony to pass. It did, grudgingly. The worst of it anyway. She was alive. She could move. The wreckage she could see scattered in the shallows was a monument to how wrong things could have gone, but she wasn’t in it.

She pushed herself forward, one hand braced against the rock, the other pressed to her ribs. The black sand clung to her wet clothes in a thousand tiny grains. She stepped out from the shelter of the rock into the pre-dawn grey. Behind the shore, the island rose in gentle slopes toward a ridge of darker rock. Green moss or lichen clung to the higher stones where the wind couldn’t scour it away. All of it bent permanently sideways by a wind that had never once let up. The sea ahead was iron-flat, still heavy with cloud-light, the rocks at the waterline slick and enormous, the kind that suggested the ocean visited regularly and on its own terms.

“You are awake.”

Sasi screamed, scrambling backward so fast her feet nearly came out from under her. She spun around. He was lying on a stone like some lounging nobleman in a drawing room. He was propped up on one elbow, watching her with an expression of mild curiosity, as though her shriek hadn’t warranted any particular response.

The breath left her entirely.

In twenty-two years aboard the ship, Sasi had developed what she privately considered a thorough catalogue of men. Sailors, traders, captains, passengers who had made a series of poor decisions and ended up at sea for their trouble. She knew the general shape of the creature.

This was not that.

“Fear not,” he lifted his head slightly, as though she’d said something when she hadn’t. “Or perhaps fear thou shouldst.”

“I…” Sasi’s voice came out as a whisper. “Ye’re stark naked, ye are.”

He hummed. His pale skin almost luminous in the pre-dawn dark. His hair was a shock of high blonde, falling like spilled silk when he rolled his defined shoulders. “False.”

Well yes, not entirely naked.

His legs were bare, and toned as was the rest of him. What draped around him was heavy and dark, some dense woven material that moved like velvet but caught the low light along its edges like frost. It covered a quantity of nothing in particular. A belt of dark links sat at his waist, small vessels hanging from it, and a length of tiny obsidian beads looped somewhere above it, and none of it was doing anything especially useful from a modesty standpoint. Sasi’s mind did several things at once. Then he rose to his feet in one fluid motion and began walking toward her, his pace unhurried and absolute.

Sasi stumbled backward. “Stay back now, keep yer distance.”

He did not stay back. Instead, he closed the distance between them with those long, measured strides, and crouched down to her level. “Are you hurt?” he asked. She shook her head. “Was that confirmation?” There it was again, that almost-smile.

“Aye,” she said quickly. “Well enough, all things considered.” He reached out a hand, held it open, patient. She stared for a moment, then her memory surfaced. A shape in the storm. A grip on her arm. “Ye pulled me from the water.”

“Yes.”

“Why? What’d ye want with the likes of me?”

He was quiet for a long moment. Long enough that Sasi began to wonder if she’d asked something offensive. His pale eyes shifted from her slightly. “I do not know.”

That wasn’t the answer she’d expected. “Well then. I be grateful anyway. I owe ye a debt. Me life for yer mercy. I’ll serve by yer side, as long as ye have need of me. That be the way of it.”

His head tilted. Then his expression shifted, just slightly, and then smoothed out again. Then the sound came. It was low enough that she felt it in her sternum before she heard it, a deep reverberation in the air between them that resolved itself.

“I am not,” he said, each word given its proper weight before release, “in need of a servant.”

Her face heated. “That weren’t what I were offerin’.”

“Mm.” He considered. “You are a peculiar existence.”

“Aye, so I’ve been told.” She crossed her arms. “Mostly by people who didn’t mean it flatterin’ly.”

“I do not speak to flatter.” He turned his face, barely, toward the water. “You offered fealty to a creature you cannot name, on a shore you do not recognise, for a debt of survival.”

“Better than drownin’, ain’t it?” Sasi said. “And ye’ve got the look of someone who can keep me alive. So aye. Better the devil ye know, or don’t know, than the water at yer back.”

He was quiet again. The sky was shifting now, that indigo becoming something slightly warmer.

“You would leave this place and follow me into uncertainty. Into waters unknown.”

“I’ve lost everything. The ship were me whole life. Twenty-two years of it,” she said. There was never much attachment to it. “Everything I knew. It be at the bottom of the sea now, and everythin’ I had with it. So unless ye not pointin’ me toward a warm meal and a soft bed, I’ll take me chances with ye.”

The black sand was beginning to show the blue-grey quality of real dawn, rather than the darkness before it. She could see the colour better in his hair now. The strands that weren’t blonde at all, but had that same iridescent quality as fish scales in weak light.

He was quiet for many breaths, then he finally spoke. “You should truly consider this. When the sun rises, you may change your mind.”

“I won’t. I don’t change me mind easy, and I don’t make promises I can’t keep.”

“Many would.”

“I ain’t many. I be Sasi, late of the Morgane, and I don’t have nowhere else to be.”

His mouth curved up at that, enough to show a flash of teeth. “Very well. If you are resolved, then I will honour your pledge.”

He held out his hand.

Sasi hesitated for only a moment before placing hers in it. He turned it over and did not look down. Then his thumb pressed to the inside of her palm and the edge of his nail opened a thin line across her skin.

“Oi.” She pulled.

His other hand closed around her wrist firmly. She jerked but he was unmoving. He brought the offending hand from her palm to his lips and bit down, then pressed the bleeding mark to hers. The ring on his hand blazed a violet-white.

“It is complete,” he released her.

“Ye gave me the fright of me bloody life,” she clutched her palm to her chest.

“That was not my intent.” A pause, careful. “Are you hurt?”

She looked at her fingertip. The line was already gone. “Nay,” she managed. “Nay, I’m right enough.”

He gently tugged the still-arrested wrist, then stopped. He made a sound, low and abrupt. He was already reaching, not to his belt, but to the bracer on his forearm. Her brows knitted, watching the veins rise on his hand as he worked something loose. Then he held it out and air escaped her. A cord. Leather. Knotted on one side where it had been cut and rebound. And on the cord, a stone, cracked clean through the middle, the two halves held together by a thin binding.

Sasi did not move.

“Where,” she said, and her voice came out wrong. “Where did ye find that.”

He said nothing. Just held it out. She took it from him, held it in both palms. The crack ran through the centre of it the way she remembered, the way it had always been. She pressed it to her chest. Sasi breathed in. In. Out. Steady.

Her diaphragm contracted anyway. The tears came then, hot and fierce and unavoidable. They poured down her face, and she couldn’t stop them, didn’t try. Her knees buckled under her and cool hands held up her last bit of dignity. Sasi leaned into him, her whole body shaking.

“Thank ye,” she choked out through the sobs. “Thank ye, thank ye…”

“Einarr.”

“Thank ye, Einarr.” She lifted her head, even so close and blurred by tears, that knit in his brows was distinct. “I don’t… I’ll follow ye. To the end of the world. I swear it. I’ll follow ye.”

He was quiet for a moment.

“Do not,” he said, “make such promises.”

“Why?” Sasi almost wiped her eyes before bringing her still-damp top to dab at it. He was looking past her. “Why not?” she asked.

He did not answer immediately.

“Because,” he said finally, each word settled carefully, “I would be obliged to keep them. Now come.”
