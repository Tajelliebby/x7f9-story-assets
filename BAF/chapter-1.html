The Grand Meridian was the kind of hotel that insisted on being noticed. Crystal chandeliers threw fractured light across marble floors, turning the annual Vela Communications recognition ceremony into something half corporate gala, half fairy tale. The speeches were over. The executives had made their exits. What remained was music and around three hundred people from three branches finally allowed to be human.

The bass was a physical thing. It lived in the sternum, in the soft place behind the ears and Haydée had finally managed to drag Camila and Jade far enough from the speakers that she could feel her own heartbeat again.

“To us!” Camila raised her glass for what had to be the sixth time this hour, champagne sloshing cheerfully over the rim and onto her hand, not like she noticed.

Haydée laughed and raised hers anyway. The fizz had gone flat but the warmth was still there, pleasantly distributed from her collarbone to her fingertips. She was tipsy, figured that out about two glasses ago and switched to careful sips while Camila graduated to full celebration mode. The Calmere branch had won again. Best quarter performance across all three offices. Camila had been competitive since their college days and apparently intended to stay that way indefinitely.

“Ladies.”

Their head of department materialized beside them, and for a split second Haydée had the distinct sensation of a student who hadn’t done anything wrong and still felt guilty about it. Ms. Patricia Osei had that effect on people. Tonight she wore emerald, her natural hair swept up in an elegant twist. Beside her stood a man with her same quality of stillness. A matched set.

“This is Robert,” Ms. Osei said, and the pride in it was quiet and real. “My husband.”

Haydée’s eyes dropped, almost reflexively. Their left pinkies were interlocked, red bands worn smooth at the base of each knuckle. Thirty-two years, those bands said. Thirty-two years and still matching.

She shook Robert’s hand when introduced. He was warm, a little formal in the way of men who had married extroverts and adapted. Jade, who had apparently decided to deploy her three-drink personality ahead of schedule, leaned in and said that Ms. Osei talked about him constantly, which turned Robert a remarkable shade of pleased and flustered that his wife observed with visible satisfaction.

Then, inevitably, Ms. Osei began to tell the story. Again.

Haydée had heard it four times since joining the Calmere office. The conference in Meridian. The misaddressed name badges. The handshake that changed everything. She smiled at the right places, laughed where she was supposed to, and let her gaze drift across the ballroom. Red bands everywhere. Couples standing close, pinkies loosely interlocked, the color catching the hotel’s ambient light like an inside joke the universe was telling in multiple languages simultaneously. Over by the far window, someone who’d had no band all evening was shaking hands with someone from the Alderton branch and Haydée watched the exact moment they both went still. The exact moment they looked down, then up at each other with expressions that had no precedent in ordinary social interaction.

She wondered what that felt like. The pivot. The before-and-after of a single handshake.

Laughter erupted somewhere to her left. Her eyes moved. And there he was. Eli Navarro stood just outside a cluster of men from marketing, slightly apart in the way he always seemed to be — present without being absorbed into the noise. His hair was slicked back tonight, different from the usual, and there was something at his eyebrow she’d never been close enough to notice before. A scar, maybe. Or a deliberate cut in the line.

The suit, though. The suit was doing something genuinely unfair.

All-black, tailored close, rhinestones scattered along the right lapel and shoulder in a constellation pattern that kept catching the light. The jacket cinched at his waist. His shoulders filled it out in a way that made her mouth go dry. One hand held a rocks glass. The other stayed tucked in his pocket.

He looks so good in that suit.

“He does,” Camila sing-songed, her voice pitching up in that way that meant she was about to cause problems. Haydée cursed her loose lips. “Go tell him.”

“I’m good.” Haydée took a careful sip of champagne. “I am perfectly content right here with both of you and this view.”

“It’s one compliment.” Jade was already crowding in from the right. Camila on the left with suspicious coordination. “What’s the worst that happens?”

Haydée narrowed her eyes, backing away. “The ground opens and swallows me—”

“—specifically and only me, in front of everyone—”

“Come on,” Camila chimed in.

The nudging had escalated to a coordinated shove and Haydée was neither as coordinated nor as resistant as she’d have liked. “No,” she whisper-hissed, trying to dig her heels in, but her shoes had exactly zero traction on the polished floor. “Stop, stop—”

One final shove and she stumbled forward, with no dignified explanation available, immediately in front of Eli Navarro and his entire friend group.

Oh my god.

Eli was watching her. His colleagues were watching her. All of them were watching her process this in real time. She looked back. Camila and Jade had turned their backs in perfect unison, shoulders already shaking.

Traitors.

“So…” Eli’s voice was low and dry, edged with something that might’ve been amusement. He tilted his head, and the rhinestones on his shoulder caught the light. “What was the bet?”

“Bet?” The word came out like a squeak. Haydée’s gaze dropped immediately to her black heels, already pinching her toes as she fought the urge to turn around and bolt. “No bet. Um.”

Oh god, she was going to throw up.

“The sparkly?” He arched a brow. Her brain was noting the scar at his eyebrow, cataloguing it, filing it away alongside two years of observations she had never had the proximity to confirm. “You a magpie?”

Eli didn’t usually talk to people at these things. He came because it was expected, stood around because it was polite, left as soon as he could without being rude. But this had been something else.
