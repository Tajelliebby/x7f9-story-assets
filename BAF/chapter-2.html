9:17 PM.

Jaydyn’s phone lit up for the eighth time in an hour. Still nothing. Just his own messages stacking up like accusations in the thread.

you good?

viv?

seriously where are you

He’d been holding the phone so long his palm was sweating. The screen went dark again, and he caught his reflection in the black glass: ears pinned back, jaw tight enough to crack a molar.

It’s fine. She’s fine. She’s with her friends, she said she’d be out, she doesn’t need to check in every five fucking seconds—

9:23 PM.

The last message from her had come at 3:47 PM. Going out with the girls! ♡

Six hours.

His tail hadn’t stopped moving since sunset, a restless sweep back and forth across the mattress that made the springs creak. The sound grated on him. Everything grated on him: his roommate’s breathing from the other side of the partition, the bass thumping through the wall from next door, the fact that his girlfriend, his mate in every way that mattered except the one that would make everyone shut the fuck up about it, hadn’t answered him in six hours.

He tried to breathe through it. Tried to remind himself Evangeline had a life outside of him, that she was allowed to be out with her friends without giving him a play-by-play, that he was being the exact kind of needy disaster she’d gently teased him about last week when he’d texted her between classes just to make sure she’d eaten.

9:31 PM.

His phone hit the wall before he’d even registered throwing it. The crack was loud enough that his roommate sat up, half-asleep and scowling. “Dude—”

“Going out.” Jaydyn was already pulling on his hoodie, shoving his feet into his boots.

“It’s almost ten, and there’s a—”

The door slammed behind him.

The cold hit him like a fist. Snow came down in sheets, heavy and wet, sticking to his eyelashes and melting on his tongue. Campus was nearly deserted, just a few stragglers hurrying toward the dorms with their heads down. The wind bit through his hoodie, but he barely felt it. Wolves ran hot.

And he was running hotter than usual.

The Greytower Café was closed, lights off, chairs stacked on tables. She’d sent him a picture of the mural behind the counter that afternoon, some latte art she was proud of, and it was the only lead he had. He checked anyway, cupping his hands against the glass to peer inside. Empty. The Ravine was a no-go in this weather; even wolves didn’t run in a storm like this unless they had a death wish. He tried the library next, the window seat from another one of her pictures, then the student center, his boots leaving muddy tracks across polished floors as he scanned every corner, every table, every face that wasn’t hers.

By the time he reached The Red Veil, his chest was tight and his hands were shaking.

The entrance was a red door tucked into an alley off The Warren, the kind of place you didn’t find unless you were looking for it. Vampire territory. He hated it on principle: the velvet ropes, the bouncers who looked at him like he’d wandered in from the gutter, the music too low, the lights too dim, everything smelling like old blood and expensive cologne.

She always sent a picture from the same corner booth when she came here, an old habit he’d never once mentioned noticing. It was the last picture he had, timestamped hours ago, and it was all he had left to go on.

But Evangeline’s friends liked it here. So he went in.

The bass was a low throb under his ribs, the kind that made his teeth ache. The main floor was packed, bodies pressed close in the half-light, and he had to shove his way through just to get to the bar. A few heads turned as he passed. A couple of vampires wrinkled their noses. One of them, some guy in a leather jacket who looked like he’d stepped out of a 1950s greaser movie, actually hissed.

Jaydyn bared his teeth and kept walking.

He found them in a corner booth near the back. Three vampires, all girls, all dressed like they were here to be seen. One of them, Margot, he thought her name was, spotted him first. Her smile was sharp. “Well, well. Look who it is.”

The others looked up. Recognition flickered across their faces, followed quickly by something else. Wariness, maybe. Amusement. Hard to tell with vampires.

Jaydyn didn’t bother with pleasantries. “Where is she?”

Margot blinked, her smile fading. “Evangeline?”

“No, the other she I’m dating.” His voice came out harsher than he’d meant it to, but he was too wound up to care. “Where’d she go?”

The girl to Margot’s left, blonde, cold-eyed, exchanged a look with the third one. “She left, like… hours ago. Said she wasn’t feeling well.”

“She wasn’t with you?”

“No?” The blonde frowned. “We figured she went to find you.”

The floor tilted. Hours ago. She’d left hours ago, and she wasn’t answering her phone, and she wasn’t in any of the places she was supposed to be, and—

“Are you okay?” Margot’s voice sounded distant, muffled, like he was hearing it through water. “You look—”

He was moving before she finished. Out of the booth, across the floor, shouldering past the crowd with enough force that someone shouted after him. He didn’t stop. Couldn’t stop. His vision was narrowing, edges going gray, chest so tight he could barely pull in air. The door slammed open. Snow hit his face. He barely felt it.

Behind him, faint but unmistakable, someone muttered just loud enough for him to hear: “I don’t know what she sees in him.”

His hands curled into fists, nails biting into his palms sharp enough to draw blood. For half a second, just half a second, he almost turned around. Almost went back in there and made them regret every word.

But Evangeline’s voice echoed in his head, soft and pleading: You get written up one more time and it’ll be months before we see each other.

He kept walking.

Her apartment was fifteen minutes away on foot. He made it in seven. Silverlight Apartments looked even more sterile in the snow, all clean lines and floor-to-ceiling windows reflecting the streetlights in cold, perfect angles. He fumbled with the spare key she’d given him three months ago, fingers clumsy with cold and adrenaline, and shouldered through the door.

The warmth inside should have been a relief. It wasn’t.

The lights were off. The apartment was silent except for the hum of the fridge and the faint howl of wind against the windows. No music. No TV. No Evangeline calling out to ask who was there.

His heart was in his throat.

“Viv?”

Nothing.

He moved through the space on instinct, eyes adjusting to the dim glow of streetlight through the curtains. The kitchenette was empty. He saw her before his brain could process what he was looking at.

She was on the floor between the couch and the coffee table, one hand stretched toward the sofa cushions like she’d been trying to pull herself up. Her legs were tucked under her, hair spilling across the tile, skin so pale it almost glowed in the half-light.

He was on his knees before he’d even registered moving.

“Evangeline, fuck, Viv, hey, hey—” His hands found her shoulders, her face, her throat where her pulse should be. It was there. Faint, slower than his, but there. Relief punched through him so hard he nearly choked on it.

He pulled her into his chest, arms locking around her like he could hold her together by sheer force of will. Her skin was cold. Too cold. Colder than usual, and usual was already cold enough to make him flinch.

“What the fuck,” he muttered into her hair, voice cracking. “What the fuck, Evangeline.”

She didn’t answer. Didn’t move. Just stayed limp against him, breath so shallow he had to press his ear to her chest to make sure she was still pulling air. The anger came back all at once, sharp and hot and overwhelming. He scooped her up, one arm under her knees, the other around her back, and carried her toward the bedroom. She was so light it made his chest ache. Too light. Too cold. Too still.

Halfway there, she stirred. “Jaydyn?”

Her voice was barely a whisper, thick with sleep and confusion. His grip tightened. “Yeah. It’s me.”

“What are you—” A pause. Her head lolled against his shoulder. “How’d you get in?”

“Spare key. You gave me a spare key, remember?” He kicked the bedroom door open, tone harsher than he meant it to be. “What the hell, Viv? You couldn’t text me? You couldn’t call?”

“My phone died.”

“Then charge it. You have a charger. You have, like, three chargers.”

“I forgot.”

He set her down harder than he should have and immediately regretted it. She blinked up at him, eyes unfocused, fingers curling weakly into the blanket. She looked so small. So fragile. It made him want to break something.

“You forgot.” He crouched to unlace her boots, yanking them off with enough force that she flinched. “You forgot to charge your phone. You forgot the portable charger your dad got you for your birthday. What else did you forget, huh? To tell me you were leaving? To let me know you were okay?”

“I didn’t think—”

“No, you didn’t.” He kicked off his own boots and climbed onto the bed beside her, pulling the covers over both of them in one sharp motion. “You didn’t think, and I spent two hours losing my mind looking for you.”

She was quiet for a long moment. When she finally spoke, her voice was so soft he almost didn’t hear it. “I’m sorry.”

The anger stuttered. Fizzled. He sighed and pulled her closer, tucking her against his chest where it was warm, where she could leech his body heat and stop being so gods-damned cold.

“You’re freezing,” he muttered.

“I’m dead.”

“Not funny.”

Her breath puffed against his collarbone, and he felt her smile. “Little bit funny.”

“Not even a little bit.” He wrapped his arms around her, one hand sliding up to cradle the back of her head. “Why didn’t you tell me you were leaving early? I would’ve come to get you.”

“It wasn’t that cold when I left.” Her words were starting to come easier now, less sluggish. His heat was working. “I didn’t think it’d drop this much.”

“You never think it’s that cold. You’re cold-blooded. You can’t tell.”

“I can tell.”

“You can tell something. You can’t tell how much.” He pressed his face into her hair, breathing in the scent of her shampoo and something else, something faintly metallic he’d learned to associate with Evangeline. “What if I hadn’t come? What if you’d just stayed on the floor all night?”

“I would’ve woken up eventually.”

“Eventually isn’t good enough.” His voice cracked again, and he hated it, hated how raw he sounded, how obvious it was that he’d been terrified. “You scared the shit out of me.”

Her fingers found his, lacing together over her stomach. “I’m okay.”

“You’re not okay. You’re half-frozen and passed out on your living room floor. That’s not okay.”

She didn’t argue. Just curled closer, legs tangling with his, cold nose pressing against the hollow of his throat. Outside, the wind howled. The snow kept falling.

“You should’ve told me where you were going,” he murmured soft.

“I did. I texted you.”

“You said you were going out. You didn’t say where. You didn’t say when you’d be back.” He tightened his hold on her. “I always tell you. I always make sure you know where I am.”

“I know.”

“So why don’t you?”

“I did. I sent you like six pictures today.”

He narrowed his eyes. “Pictures aren’t the same as telling me where you are.”

“They’re literally pictures of where I am.”

“They’re pictures of a mural, and a window seat, and some booth.” He stared at the ceiling instead of her. “None of it tells me if you’re still there three hours later. I was just following where you used to be.”

“I didn’t think it mattered to you.”

He stared at her. “Are you serious right now?”

“You’re always saying you don’t need to know every little thing I do.”

“That’s different.”

“How?”

“Because I’m trying not to be overbearing! I’m trying not to be the obsessive boyfriend who needs a fucking GPS tracker on you!” His voice rose, and he forced himself to take a breath. His tail whipped left and right.

She went quiet.

“You like going where the night takes you. I’m not going to be the reason you stop, so I don’t ask.” He kept going. “Doesn’t mean I’m not thinking about it.”

“You don’t seem like you’re thinking about it. You seem like you don’t care where I am. Like it doesn’t register.”

“That’s not—” He stopped, actually stopped, because under the blanket his tail had gone rigid, thumping once, hard, against the mattress.

She felt it. Her eyes dropped to where the shape of it was outlined under the covers, still now, caught. He said nothing.

“Jaydyn.”

“It does that sometimes, okay?” He sat up on his elbow to look at her, and she let him, but her eyes went somewhere over his shoulder instead of meeting his. “Look, I just want to know you’re breathing.”

“I was breathing. Barely, but.”

“Viv.”

“Sorry.” She said it too fast, too light.

He almost let her. He was tired, and she was warm now, finally, and the easy thing was to let her deflect and go to sleep angry-adjacent like always. But something about the way she said it snagged on him.

“You keep doing that,” he said.

“Doing what.”

He gestured vaguely. “You make it a bit.” She was quiet long enough that he thought she wasn’t going to answer. “Come on, babe, talk to me.”

“Sometimes I think it’d be easier,” she said, to the ceiling, not to him, “if I just stopped fighting for a parking spot I’m not sure exists.”

He turned that over. “What’s the parking spot.”

“Nothing. Forget it.”

“Evangeline.”

“It’s not about you, okay? It’s not. I just meant, generally, in the abstract, that maybe some things aren’t—” She stopped. Pressed her mouth shut like she could physically stop the rest of the sentence from getting out.

“Say it.”

“No.”

“Say it, or I’m going to lie here assuming the worst version of whatever it is.”

She turned her head and looked at him then, and her jaw was doing the thing it did right before she cried, tight and trembling at the hinge. “Your pack didn’t say a word to me at the bonfire. Not one word. Six weeks, and they still look at me like I’m going to bite one of you.”

“Viv—”

“I’m not finished.” Her voice shook, but she kept going. “And my coven had a whole conversation about you at dinner last month like I wasn’t sitting right there, and the dean’s office has your name in a folder now, an actual folder, because of me, because of the fight you got in defending me, and I just—” She sucked in a breath. “I keep doing the math. On whether it’s worth it. What it’s costing you.”

“Don’t do that math.”

“I can’t help it, it’s just there, it does itself—”

“Then stop.” He caught her face in both hands, not gentle about it.

“You say that like it’s simple.”

“It’s not simple. None of it’s simple.” His thumbs pressed into her cheekbones, and his voice dropped, rougher. “You want to know what my dad said to me. Last time I called him.”

She went still. “You never told me you called him.”

“Because you’d have done exactly this. That face.” He exhaled hard through his nose. “He asked if I was tired yet.”

“Tired.”

“Of it. Of you. Of the whole thing.” His jaw ticked. “Like it’s a phase. Like I’m going to wake up one morning and go, huh, weird, guess I’m done.”

“Are you?”

“Am I what—” He caught himself, caught the actual question underneath it, and something in his chest went sideways. “No. Christ, Viv, no.”

“You don’t have to say it like that just because I asked.”

“I’m not saying it like anything. I’m saying no.” He pulled her hand off the blanket and pressed it flat against his sternum, over the heartbeat going too fast to be casual about it. “Feel that. That’s not a guy who’s tired.”

She didn’t pull her hand back. She left it there, and her breathing did something uneven. “I keep waiting for the version of you that’s tired,” she admitted, so quiet it barely made it across the pillow. “I keep bracing for it.”

“Well, stop. You’ll be bracing a long time.”

“You don’t know that.”

“I know I haven’t left yet. Through all of it. That has to count for something.” His forehead dropped to hers, and for a second neither of them said anything at all, just breathed the same air in the dark. “You planning on going anywhere?”

“No.”

“Then quit,” he shuffled, “looking for the end.”

She searched his face. “You still hate everyone equally?”

He looked at her for a long moment. Studied the way her eyes caught the faint glow of the streetlights, the way her lips trembled like she was holding back something too big to name.

“No,” he said finally.

She let out something between a laugh and a sob, and he felt it against his mouth before he heard it. “I love you,” she said, like it had gotten loose before she could stop it, and immediately looked like she wanted to take it back.

He didn’t let her. “Say it again.”

“Jaydyn—”

“Say it again, I want to hear it when it’s not an accident.”

“I love you.” Steadier this time, and terrified, but steadier. “There. Happy?”

“No.” He kissed her, slow, like he had all the time in the world even though he’d spent the last two hours convinced he had none. When he pulled back, his voice had gone rough in a different way. “I love you too. Have for a while. Wasn’t going to say it first because you’d have made it weird.”

“I would not have.”

“You made my tail weird.”

She laughed again, wet and real this time, and pressed her face into his neck so he couldn’t see her cry properly, which was somehow the most honest thing she’d done all night. “When we’re old,” she started, then stopped, huffing at herself. “You know what, forget it, vampires don’t—”

“I’m planning on it anyway.”

She pulled back to look at him properly. “Planning on what.”

“Sticking around. Whatever old looks like for us.” No hesitation. “You got that?”

That was all. But it was everything.

She kissed him before he could say anything else, hands fisting in his hoodie, mouth desperate and salt-wet and his. He kissed her back just as hard, fingers tightening in her hair, heart pounding loud enough he could hear it in his ears.

And then he felt it.

A pull. Not physical, but something deeper, something primal and undeniable that made his wolf sit up and take notice. Their scents were blending, her cold-blood-and-old-books mixing with his rain-and-leather until he couldn’t tell where one ended and the other began. His skin prickled, warmth blooming across his collarbone, spreading down toward his chest.

She gasped and pulled back, eyes wide. “Jaydyn—”

“I feel it too.” His hand caught hers before she could pull further away, and that’s when he saw it: a thin red band, wrapped around her pinkie like it had always been there.

She followed his stare down to her own hand, then to his, where the same band sat glowing faintly against his skin. Between them, barely visible but undeniably there, a thread of translucent red stretched taut.

“We—” Her voice cracked. “We’re—”

“Soulmates.” The word felt foreign on his tongue. Impossible.

“But we met freshman year. We touched. We—” She laughed, the sound wet and breathless. “I can’t believe, we’ve been—”

“I know.”

“And we didn’t even—”

“I know.” He kissed her again, softer this time. Reverent. “But we do now.”

She pulled back just enough to meet his eyes. Her eyes filled with tears again, but this time they spilled over, tracking down her cheeks in twin lines. “They were wrong.”

“Yeah.”

“They were wrong about us.”

“Yeah.” He pulled her closer, forehead dropping to hers. “So fucking wrong.” He held her face a second longer, thumb brushing her cheekbone, grounding both of them. And that was when he saw it, just above the neckline of her shirt: a mark. Faint, still forming, but unmistakable, a crescent-shaped impression pressed into her pale skin like a seal into wax.

His mark. His claim.

“Viv.” His voice dropped. “Don’t move.”

“What—”

He didn’t answer. He leaned in and closed his teeth gently over the mark instead, a slow, soft nip. The sound she made wasn’t quite a gasp, her fingers digging into his shoulders.

“That mark,” she breathed. “That’s yours.”

“Mine.” He said it against her skin, rough and low.

She dragged his face up to hers and kissed him before he could say anything else, hands fisting in his hoodie, mouth desperate and salt-wet and his. He kissed her back just as hard, fingers tightening in her hair, heart pounding loud enough he could hear it in his ears. Then, slowly, deliberately, she leaned in and pressed her mouth to the side of his neck. He felt her fangs a second before they broke skin. The pain was sharp and bright, but it faded almost immediately, replaced by something warmer. Something that felt like coming home. She drank slowly, carefully, her hand splayed over his chest to feel his heartbeat. When she finally pulled away, he saw it: the mark her bite had left behind, red and raised and permanent.

A scar. His scar. Hers.

The string between them flared bright for just a moment, then settled into a steady glow. Outside, the storm raged on. But inside, wrapped around each other in a nest of blankets and body heat, they were warm.

They were safe.

They were theirs.
