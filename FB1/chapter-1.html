Rurik stood frozen at the balcony's edge, his fingers gripping the cold stone as if it might anchor him to sanity. The woman before him, whom he had first mistaken for an errant child, grinned at him. "Do you think she'll be angry?" Green eyes crinkled as she repeated the question.

A tightness gripped his chest, a sense of... surprise, maybe? Before he could master it, a voice cut through the night air. "Adelaide?" The tone was rougher than he expected.

The golden-haired creature to his right, for surely she was some manner of divine test, pressed gloved hands against her mouth to stifle another bout of laughter. The evening breeze stirred her hair like wheat in a summer field, and he noted with growing disbelief the fresh flowers woven through those waves. Her gown, which must have cost more than a month's wages for his border guards, bore testament to her complete disregard for propriety as she crouched, merciful Light, she was crouching among the perfectly manicured rose bushes.

What manner of madness had the One Light seen fit to present him with?

His gaze shifted to the figure prowling the garden's edge. An auburn-haired woman stood below, her hair catching the moonlight like autumn leaves touched by frost. Her eyes, remarkable things flecked with molten gold, scanned the garden. "Adelaide," she called again, "pray tell, what manner of game are you playing? We are expected to make our entrance to the ballroom momentarily."

Despite himself, Rurik found his curiosity piqued. These women were separate from the mindless peacocks that filled the Hall of Flames, their attire spoke of wealth, yet lacked the gaudy excess favoured by the court's desperate social climbers. The auburn-haired woman carried herself with something that gave him pause, while her companion seemed... irrepressible.

Adelaide caught his gaze again, unlike the ladies of the court who wilted under his attention, she met it without hesitation. "I think Sister Rowena might scold me later." The words 'Why then persist in this folly?' died on his tongue as she continued, "I wonder how long before she calls our brothers. Brother Kaelen is always so good at hide and seek, it's most unfair."

Who was this woman? The nobility he knew were like carved ice: beautiful, cold, and utterly predictable in their pursuit of power. Yet here was one playing hide and seek like a common child, with not a thought for her station. Rowena drew closer, and he stepped back instinctively, though he could not tear his gaze away. Adelaide began to inch around the bush, attempting to slip past her pursuer with all the stealth of a drunken guardsman.

"There you are!" Rowena's voice held more warmth than rebuke. "You imp."

Adelaide's squeak of surprise dissolved into musical laughter. "Sister Rowena! You found me."

Rowena's sighed, her lips twitched with poorly concealed amusement. "Why did I have to search for you in the first place, Della? We're too old to be playing hide and seek."

Adelaide rose, brushing at her skirts even as her lips stretched with glee. "One can never be too old for hide and seek."

May the Light forgive him, but Rurik felt his own lips threaten to curve upward.

Rowena's eyes narrowed with playful suspicion. "Who set you up for this? After you promised Elaine to be on your best behaviour. I shall tell your nanny when we return."

Adelaide's gasp of horror was somewhat undermined by her increasing mirth. "No, no! You wouldn't..."

"No need to tell Elaine, I found the culprit."

The voice drew Rurik's attention like a blade being unsheathed. Its owner stood like living marble carved by divine hands: towering, austere, crowned with hair as white as temple snow. His ice-blue eyes held the same cutting clarity as the frozen rivers of home, and the air around him carried winter's bite. Even from his sheltered position on the balcony, Rurik felt the familiar chill of northern power.

Thorne. Though they'd never met, whispers of his reputation echoed through Aurenfell's halls. Another son of the north, though the capital had claimed him young.

His long fingers pinched the ear of a man slightly shorter than himself. "Ow, ow!" The captive yelped, his broad shoulders bunching beneath fine cloth. Sun-bronzed skin and hair like holy fire marked him as Thorne's natural opposite. Amber eyes watered as he pleaded, "Let go of my ear!"

"Oh heavens," Adelaide's whisper carried the lightness of spring rain, "Brother Thorne has caught Brother Kaelen."

Rurik's lips betrayed him with another unwanted smile at her utter lack of concern for this display of unseemly behaviour.

Thorne released his prey with an unimpressed grunt. "Kaelen, are your nerves still troubling you?" His tone softened without losing authority. "Surely you recall this is hardly our first attendance at such an affair."

Lord Kaelen straightened his collar with the wounded dignity of a wet cat. "Indeed, I am well aware," he muttered, "I merely required a moment more to compose myself."

Rowena rested a hand on his shoulder. "Kaelen..."

Kaelen let out a quiet huff, eyes on his polished shoes. The attempt at heavy silence was thwarted the moment Adelaide grinned. "As we are all presently assembled, shall we make our entrance?"

Thorne's sigh told Rurik of countless similar surrenders. "Very well. Let us proceed. Though I must insist, no more dallying in the gardens."

As they turned toward the warmth and music spilling from the ballroom, Adelaide paused. Her gaze swept the courtyard one final time before finding his shadow-shrouded position with unerring accuracy. That same impish creature who had crawled through perfectly manicured gardens with such abandon, raised her hand in greeting. The gesture was so utterly unexpected, he froze. Her eyes held his until he found himself offering a single, stiff nod. She smiled again, bright and unbothered, and then she was gone, the sound of her light footsteps fading into the distance as she rejoined her peculiar family.

Such an odd experience...

Rurik blinked at the darkened sky as if the stars might offer guidance. The Light's will demanded his return to that gilded cage of expectations, though the garden scene had granted him a moment's blessed respite. With one final prayer for strength, he turned back toward the ever-watching eyes within.

"Duke Aurenfell! There you are, my boy!"

His shoulders tensed. No, please no.

He turned, forcing his features into the mask expected of his station. Duke Lawson's robust frame blocked his escape route, his jovial manner melting some of the ice in his veins as he clasped his shoulder. Beside him, Viscount Eve offered a more reserved greeting.

"By God, Your Grace!" Duke Lawson's voice boomed like temple bells across the hushed floor. "If only your father could see you now. You've grown into a true wolf of the North!" His meaty hand gestured at his frame as if appraising a prized warhorse.

"Indeed," Viscount Eve's cultured tones carried the weight of memory. "The very image of Lord Aldric in his prime."

Rurik inclined his head, feeling the weight of their scrutiny heavy as a Templar's judgment. Always measuring, always comparing, seeking his father's shadow in his flesh. But I am not him. "The weight of legacy is heavy for all of us, Your Grace," he murmured, fighting the urge to wrap his cloak around himself.

The conversation meandered through safer waters, state matters, the lifeblood of noble gatherings. "The northern trade routes," Duke Lawson leaned close, his wine-sweet breath mixing with the cloying incense. "I hear you've finally cleared them of those wretched bandits."

Rurik offered the ghost of a smile. "Necessity breeds action, my lord. The mountain passes were becoming impossible for merchants."

"Might I suggest something more permanent?" Viscount Eve stroked his beard like a priest contemplating scripture. "A proper road through the northern territories."

Duke Lawson's laugh echoed off the gilt-edged ceiling. "Capital idea! What say you, Your Grace?"

He hummed, letting their words wash over him. Only offering the occasional grunt of acknowledgement. His spirit had already fled back to the tundra's blessed silence, where perfume and silk gave way to honest ice and steel. But then...

"Speaking of permanent arrangements," Duke Lawson's tone shifted like a serpent preparing to strike, "have you yet found yourself a suitable match?"

A muscle twitched near Rurik's temple, the only crack in his carefully maintained façade. This trial again, he thought, as if my worth is measured only in my ability to continue a bloodline.

"The roads are safe for now," he quickly tried to steer back to safer waters. "Any infrastructure improvements can wait."

Lawson chuckled the sound grating against his nerves. "We're simply curious, Aurenfell. We want to see you settled as your father would have wanted. You've been to enough battles," he continued, his smile now a weapon. "Surely someone has caught your eye?"

His skin prickled as if touched by unholy magic. "That's not why I'm here, Your Grace. I'm here on matters of state. Nothing more."

Lawson hummed, sensing his discomfort, and Viscount Eve joined in with a knowing smile. "Ah, but surely even you must acknowledge the empire's interest in your future. There's more to leadership than war, you know."

The cold in his chest spread like corruption from the Rift, pressing against his ribs with physical force. "I'll let you know when I find someone worthy," he muttered, fixing his gaze on the eternal flame where the phoenix danced. Its light offered no comfort but reminded him of his duty as a son of Aurenfell: a path of fire he must walk, willing or not.

He'd rather face a thousand Aedra than endure this farce.

Their well-meaning platitudes continued until Eve's voice turned triumphant. "Delphine! Faye! Come meet the Duke of Aurenfell."

Rurik's lips twisted downward as the Viscount's wife approached, leading not a daughter but a hunting party. Their gazes raked across him like claws seeking purchase.

"Your Grace, the Duke of Aurenfell," Lady Faye's voice dripped honeyed poison as she curtsied, "we've heard such remarkable tales of your recent triumph. Pray, might you regale us with the details of your heroic endeavours?"

Their voices blurred like a distant storm, drowned by the war drums of his own pulse. The perfume roses, lavender, and Light knows what else crashed against him in suffocating waves. How many hours had they spent preparing for this assault? How many rehearsals of these practised smiles?

His stomach roiled. Light preserve me, I think I'm about to be sick.

"I..." The silk at his throat became a noose, each breath a battle against rising panic.

A lace-adorned hand reached for him like a striking viper. The mere thought of contact sent tremors through his frame.

I can't do this.

He shifted away from the threatened touch just as salvation arrived in the herald's booming voice: "His Majesty, Emperor Ardentis Valhedran the Third!"

"If you'll excuse me," he managed through clenched teeth, executing a tactical retreat. His hand fumbled for a handkerchief, a futile shield against the bile rising in his throat. He sought higher ground, ascending to the second level as silence fell like a temple shroud. The bannister became his anchor as he fought for breath, rage and nausea warring in his gut.

Below, the royal family processed into view. The emperor, standing just slightly taller than his six-foot-two frame, a towering and battle-hardened figure, exuded authority with every step, his dark mahogany eyes sweeping the room. At his side, Queen Elowen embodied regal elegance, her crimson and white gown flowing like silk. Her skin was porcelain-pale, her auburn hair swept up in an intricate coiffure and her sharp emerald gaze missing nothing.

Crown Prince Cassius mirrored his father's bearing. His posture already spoke of future glory, shoulders squared beneath the weight of destiny. In contrast, Princess Lyria, delicate and introspective, observed the gathering with a contemplative air. Their attire rich crimson, pristine white, and gold embroidery. The phoenix rose eternal on their capes, its wings spread in triumph, a testament to Valhedran's divine right to rule.

At least, he thought grimly, their entrance has drawn the vultures' attention from me.
