The carriage wheels crunched through snow as Harald’s voice boomed across the frozen courtyard, announcing their arrival with the ceremonial weight befitting a duke’s return. “His Grace, Duke of Aurenfell, returns to Icescar!”

The inner gates creaked open, a decorative echo of the great Isgaldr Gate behind them, their ancient hinges protesting against the bitter cold. Ice crystals caught the torchlight, making the runes carved into the black stone shimmer like frozen starlight. Adelaide pressed her face against the carriage window, her breath fogging the glass as she watched the walls part to reveal the town beyond.

“Oh!” she gasped, nearly bouncing in her seat as the carriage lurched forward into the snowy streets. “Look at them all!”

Even in the early dark, with lanterns glowing along every stoop, children bundled in thick furs still played in the snow, their laughter echoing off the stone buildings. Shopkeepers swept their steps while others shuttered their windows against the night, all pausing to bow respectfully as the ducal carriage passed. Warm light spilled from tavern windows, and the scent of roasted meat and pine smoke drifted through the air. Adelaide fumbled with the window latch, ignoring Elaine’s horrified gasp as cold air rushed into the carriage. Her cheeks immediately flushed pink, but her green eyes sparkled with pure delight.

“My Lady, please!” Elaine protested, reaching for the window.

Rurik leaned forward in his saddle with concern. “Lady Adelaide, you’ll catch your death. Please, stay inside the carriage and keep warm until we reach the keep.” He called from his horse, his black cloak, stiff with dried snow and mud from their earlier battle, stirring behind him. The cold gnawed at him through the wool, and he regretted not wearing an undercoat beneath it.

“Absolutely not,” she declared, craning her neck to see better. “Tell me about your home! This is all so beautiful.”

Despite his worry, something loosened in Rurik’s chest at her obvious wonder. He’d feared she might find the north too harsh, too different from the gentle warmth of the south. Rurik guided his horse closer to the carriage.

“What you’re seeing is Vanguard Plaza,” he explained, following her gaze to the bustling square. “Most of these buildings house shops, taverns, and eating houses. Commerce, mainly: easier for traders and citizens alike, since this is the only flat ground for quite some distance. On feast days, it is also the muster ground for our assemblies.” He pointed toward distant lights that climbed the mountainside. “There are other districts connected to the main town, but we won’t be going that way tonight.”

As their carriage began the winding ascent toward Frostspire Keep, Adelaide noticed how the road grew lonelier, the warm lights of the town falling away below them. The path curved around the mountain’s base, flanked by towering pines. Soon they passed beneath the towering archways of Frostspire’s gates and rolled into the courtyard, where lantern-bearing servants stood in the snow. An elderly man stepped forward with ramrod-straight posture and the unmistakable bearing of northern nobility.

“Welcome home, Your Grace,” he said, his voice smooth but edged with concern. Beside him, a younger man with sandy hair and sharp grey eyes offered a crooked grin.

“Joachim,” Rurik nodded, then narrowed his eyes at the sandy-haired man. “Henrik.”

“Your Grace.” Henrik’s grin widened innocently.

At the edge of the lantern-light, one of the maids stood a little apart from the others, wringing her apron, her eyes moving anxiously from rider to rider as the weary party filed in. Rurik dismounted and drew Harald and his knights aside. “Have the fallen carried in with honour,” he murmured, “and see their families sent for.”

Harald bowed his head, and Rurik turned back to the household, he saw the exact moment she stopped looking. With a resigned sigh, he moved to the carriage door and helped Adelaide down, his touch brief but careful. Behind them, Maris and Cecily climbed from the second carriage, and Elaine joined them.

“May I present my betrothed, Lady Adelaide Veridis,” he announced to the assembled household.

Adelaide stepped forward with a graceful curtsy, her golden curls bouncing as she rose. “Good evening. I am Adelaide Veridis.” Her voice carried warmth and genuine curiosity as her emerald eyes took in the assembled household.

Joachim led them inside. In the first hall, a long carpet ran from the doors toward the great stairs, and servants stood in two neat rows on either side of it, heads bowed. What truly caught Adelaide’s attention wasn’t the impressive architecture or the knights’ disciplined poses, but the servants themselves. Nearly all of them were men. She blinked in surprise, and she could see her three maids exchanging puzzled glances as they too registered the unusual composition of the household staff. Among them, a young footman stepped forward.

“Your Ladyship,” he said, presenting a beautifully carved wooden chest, its surface etched with intricate patterns reminiscent of frost on window panes. “The household staff has prepared this modest token to welcome you to your new residence.”

Warmth bloomed in Adelaide’s chest at this unexpected kindness. She accepted the chest with hands that trembled slightly. The moment she lifted the ornate lid, an extraordinary fragrance overwhelmed her senses: sharp notes of mountain pine mingled with delicate northern wildflowers, all underlaid with something richer.

“Oh! How absolutely marvellous!” A sound of pure delight escaped her lips before she could moderate her reaction. “These scents… they’re crafted from your northern blooms, are they not?”

The footman nodded, a pleased look flickering across his face. “Yes, My Lady.”

“I shall treasure this always,” she declared, holding the small box close to her chest, savouring the lingering fragrance even after closing the lid. Her heightened senses allowed her to feel the scents clinging to her, and she knew that even without opening it again, the perfume would stay with her for hours. When Elaine stepped forward expectantly, Adelaide shook her head slightly, unwilling to part with the gift just yet.

“I presume some preparations have been made?” Rurik spoke quietly to Joachim. Only then did Joachim approach Adelaide.

Every aspect of his appearance spoke of precise control, from his perfectly starched cravat to the measured way he held his hands clasped before him. “I am Joachim, Your Ladyship, His Grace’s chamberlain,” he announced, each word crisp as new-fallen snow. “If you would permit me, I shall escort you to your private chambers.”

Adelaide studied him for a moment, sensing the faintest ripple of something beneath his formal demeanour. Disapproval? Wariness? The tension in his shoulders and the careful blankness of his expression were fascinating in their restraint.

Still, she pushed her curiosity aside and offered him her most radiant smile. “The pleasure is entirely mine, dear Joachim.”

Beyond the entrance hall, the great hall of Frostspire swallowed them in its deep chill as they passed beneath the towering archways. Banners hung solemnly from the high rafters, their fabric embroidered with the sigils and victories of Rurik’s ancestors. Relics of battles long past lined the walls: old swords, battered shields, and frost-rimed war horns, each whispering stories of triumph that now seemed distant. The grand hall stretched toward infinity, its dark beams carved with elaborate designs. The walls were draped in deep blues and silvers, and the soft clink of armour echoed through the space as knights stood at silent attention. It reminded her, somewhat, of its master: imposing, beautiful, and carefully controlled.

“Oh, heavens,” she breathed, unable to contain her wonder as her eyes traced every detail.

From the ancient granite walls that seemed to grow from the very mountain itself to the intricate ice-like patterns carved into the battlements, there was something utterly magnificent in its unyielding nature. So different from the gentle curves and organic shapes of southern design, yet no less enchanting.

A sneeze escaped her, and Rurik’s hand lifted halfway toward his cloak before he remembered it could not offer her more. He looked at the servants lining the carpet and said curtly, “A fur for Lady Adelaide. At once.” A footman hurried away and returned with a heavy pelt, and Elaine took it and settled it around Adelaide’s shoulders herself. It was warm and smelled of pine and cedar.

“Thank you,” Adelaide beamed up at him.

His fingers twitched and he quickly looked away. “Are the baths prepared?” Rurik asked.

Joachim nodded. “This way, Your Grace.”

The chamberlain led them through winding hallways, three other servants trailing quietly behind. Adelaide’s eyes wandered constantly, taking in the beauty of carved stone walls that seemed almost alive with intricate vine patterns, and wall hangings that depicted ancient battles and triumphs. The further they went, the more she felt like she was walking through living history, one steeped in shadow and power.

They approached a set of ornate doors. “The Duchess’s chambers have been prepared for Your Ladyship,” Joachim announced.

Adelaide looked surprised. Since they weren’t yet married, she hadn’t expected to be given such a prestigious room. She studied the door curiously before Rurik reached past her to open it. The room before her was nothing short of enchanting, a perfect blend of northern craftsmanship and subtle luxury. Rustic yet undeniably refined, with smooth stone walls contrasted by finely carved wooden beams overhead. Pale wall hangings depicted intricate floral patterns in soft blues, creams, and silvery whites.

The air smelled faintly of pine and wildflowers, lilies, heathers, and blooms she didn’t recognize, all placed carefully in delicate vases throughout the room. The scent was fresh and comforting, a stark contrast to the heavy perfumes of the capital. Near the hearth, a stack of firewood waited, adding to the sense of quiet warmth.

Her eyes were drawn to the large canopy bed that dominated the room. The frame was dark, polished wood, and the linen curtains that draped from it were light and airy, softening the space. But it was the grand window that truly caught her breath: framed by delicate curtains, it overlooked the small town below and the vast northern wilderness beyond. Snow dusted the rooftops, silvered by lantern-glow, and the horizon beyond the black line of the mountains was a deep indigo rimmed with the last faint line of violet.

“How lovely,” she murmured, her voice barely above a whisper. The room was so different from the polished opulence of the capital, quieter, gentler, but no less beautiful.

Joachim motioned to two women who had quietly entered the room. The chamberlain nodded respectfully and took his leave, while a male servant set down her luggage with efficient, cautious movements. Adelaide turned her attention to the maids, a gentle smile curving her lips.

“Pray tell, what are your names?”

The brief glance they shared suggested her directness was perhaps unexpected. The younger of the two gathered her courage. “If it pleases Your Ladyship, I am Clara.”

“And I am Astrid, Your Ladyship,” the elder woman added, her spine straight as an ancient oak but her expression softening. The keys at her waist jingled softly as she executed a perfect curtsy. “As head housekeeper, I shall oversee the daily management of your chambers and personal comfort.”

Adelaide’s face lit up with genuine delight. “I am most thoroughly pleased to be your Lady. I place myself entirely in your capable hands.” She turned to the stone fireplace, her fingers flicking toward the hearth. The logs began to glow with a soft golden light, warmth spreading through the room as if from a small, gentle sun. “To have arranged all this with such haste, I cannot express the depth of my gratitude.” Adelaide quickly settled before the fire, but Elaine’s sharp voice cut through her contentment.

“My Lady, you will not sit upon the floor like a common child.” When Adelaide pouted, Elaine turned to the northern maids with authority. “Do not coddle her, or she will become even more undignified than this. She requires firm guidance.”

Adelaide found herself manoeuvred into a warm bath, the water perfumed with calming herbs, steam curling off the surface. After the bath, they dried her hair and were in the middle of brushing it when a knock came at the door. Elaine gave permission to enter, and Joachim appeared.

“Your Ladyship, would you care to join His Grace for dinner?”

“Is the Duke dining now?” Adelaide asked.

“Yes, Your Ladyship,” Joachim answered, though he looked distinctly displeased.

Adelaide studied his expression with direct curiosity. “What is the matter, Joachim?”

The question seemed to startle him, but regaining his composure, he lowered his voice. “Your Ladyship, I am certain this arrangement was made by His Imperial Majesty, so I shall offer you a bit of counsel.” He paused, choosing his words carefully. “His Grace has made it clear that he… prefers to avoid physical contact with women. He is… sensitive to such matters. For that reason, I advise you to approach him only when absolutely necessary.”

Adelaide paused, then her face lit up with understanding. “Oh! That explains so much. Thank you for telling me.”

Her brightness seemed to catch Joachim off guard. “You are… not troubled by this information?”

“Not at all. Now then, I shall join him for dinner directly.” She turned to Astrid with sparkling eyes. “Would you help me look my best for dinner?”

Joachim felt rather dumbfounded as Elaine approached the door with a knowing smirk. “My Lady needs to dress now, so if you would excuse us,” she said pleasantly, closing the door on the bewildered chamberlain.

Just as the door clicked shut, Rurik emerged from his own chambers. Seeing Joachim standing outside Adelaide’s door, he raised an eyebrow. “What are you doing here?”

“Simply informing Her Ladyship about dinner arrangements, Your Grace.”

“Is she coming?”

“Yes, Your Grace.” Joachim froze, removed his spectacles, and looked at Rurik again with sharp attention.

“What?” Rurik arched a brow at the strange behaviour.

“Oh, nothing, Your Grace,” Joachim answered quickly, replacing his spectacles. For just a moment, he could have sworn he’d seen Rurik smiling, actually smiling, but surely he must have been mistaken. The Duke never smiled.
