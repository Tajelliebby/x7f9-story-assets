Emperor Ardentis stepped forward, his voice thundering through the Hall of Flames like a war drum. “Distinguished nobles, honoured guests! We gather not for mere revelry, but to celebrate a most magnificent triumph of the realm!”

Beyond the great doors, Adelaide stood beside Kaelen, his sweaty palm pressed against hers. His anxiety crawled across her skin like ice, making her stomach flutter in sympathy. Poor brother. He could face down a dozen Aedra without blinking, but put him in front of nobles with their painted smiles and sharp tongues, and he crumbled.

“You shall do very well,” she whispered, letting warmth flow through her touch—not power, just comfort. “You can do all things through Him that strengthens us, yes?” She reached up to smooth the deep furrow between his brows. “You shall furrow that brow permanently if you keep at it.”

A smile ghosted across his face, tension melting from his shoulders. “Thank you.”

“You are most welcome!” She beamed, counting this small victory among her greatest achievements.

The Emperor’s voice boomed again through the ornate doors: “And now, I bid you welcome our guests of honour. The distinguished members of the Sacred Order of the Seasons1. Pray, join me in receiving them into our company.”

Kaelen sucked in a sharp breath as the double doors groaned open. Music cascaded out, strings and woodwinds weaving together like golden thread. The scent of perfume and wine and a hundred different dishes hit Adelaide’s senses all at once.

Adelaide stepped onto the cold marble first, chin lifted, shoulders back. Her plain travelling gown began its transformation the moment they crossed the threshold. Delicate pastel pink unfurled like petals awakening to morning light, gossamer-thin white layers dancing with vines that pulsed with living intention. Flowers bloomed along her seams—not embroidered, but truly alive. Each petal caught the chandelier light and threw it back in soft, spring colours.

Behind her, Kaelen’s cape suddenly blazed. Holy fire licked along the edges, flames that burned without consuming. His delighted chuckle preceded the sacred roar, and Adelaide’s joy spilled over until creation itself responded. Dormant roses burst forth in crimson glory along the marble columns, violets unfurled their shy petals through the cracks in the floor, and the very air hummed with fragrant anticipation.

“You have done it!” she whispered.

“I have done it,” he breathed.

They stepped aside, allowing their siblings to follow. Thorne entered next, winter incarnate in human form. His ice-diamond regalia refracted the chandeliers’ golden glow into dazzling frost-like radiance that made Adelaide squint. Gauntlets and boot cuffs that seemed sculpted from crystalline ice caught the light like a thousand frozen stars. Each step he took left tiny crystals of frost on the marble. At his side, Rowena strode forward like autumn wind made flesh. Her gown flowed like molten gold; the fabric seemed alive, pulsing and shifting as though it danced.

They assembled in their designated positions, Adelaide’s pulse quickening as Thorne advanced to make the formal presentation before the royal family. She caught sight of Princess Lyria, sweet-faced and barely sixteen, with curious dark eyes. She couldn’t contain herself and lifted her hand in an eager wave. The princess returned it with a bright smile.

The Emperor’s voice, rich and commanding, echoed through the chamber. “We thank you for your continued efforts and congratulate you on the successful recapture and restoration of the seven counties of Risenholt to their rightful governance. This victory shall be recorded in the annals of history as a testament to our great nation’s strength. Now, rest and take your ease at the banquet held in your honour, for you must surely be weary from your labours.”

Thorne accepted the honour with quiet dignity befitting his station, inclining his head just so. Polite applause rippled through the grand hall like wind through wheat. But Adelaide barely heard it. Her attention had already wandered to Princess Lyria again, who was still grinning at her from behind her father’s ceremonial cape. Rowena noticed Adelaide’s distraction and moved to catch her arm, but she was too late. Adelaide—impulsive, bright Adelaide—had already taken a running start. Her feet barely touched the floor as she skipped toward the royal dais, arms outstretched like a child greeting family.

“Your Imperial Majesties are most gracious to host us!” she declared.

She opened her palms, letting golden light shimmer between them. Gentle fragrance wove through the air—rose and jasmine, violet and lilies—as petals began to form from pure radiance. Each blossom was a different shade, each one catching the crystal chandeliers’ glow until a perfect bouquet lay cradled in her hands. With a bright, unwavering smile, she presented the flowers to the royal couple. Gasps murmured through the gathering. Adelaide heard the sharp intake of breath from at least a dozen courtiers, the rustle of silk as people leaned forward or stepped back. But the royal family’s response was far from scandalized.

Emperor Ardentis and Empress Elowen exchanged amused glances, the look of parents watching a beloved but unpredictable child. The queen’s expression warmed as she accepted the divine bouquet with grace that came from decades of practice.

“What a thoughtful gesture, dear child,” she chuckled, her voice carrying genuine affection.

Adelaide’s entire being seemed to bloom with that simple acceptance. The flowers woven through her golden hair began to open wider. Then her eyes flickered up, feeling a pointed gaze from the upper balcony. There, the knight from the garden. Steel-grey eyes watched her with an intensity that made her stomach flutter. She grinned at him, feeling her nose scrunch up. His dark brow rose, but she could have sworn she saw his lips twitch. Almost a smile. Delighted laughter bubbled up from her chest.

What an interesting fellow indeed.

Thorne’s voice cut through her wandering thoughts, calling her back to protocol. She turned and trotted after her siblings like an obedient duckling, still grinning. The court orchestra began the opening strains of the ceremonial waltz. Time for the Archons to dance, a tradition as old as the Order itself, meant to display their divine favour before the assembled nobility.

Thorne led the procession, his ice-diamond accoutrements catching the light like a thousand frozen stars as he offered his arm to Rowena. They moved together with mature, practised grace, every step precise, every turn flowing with effortless poise. Kaelen offered Adelaide his arm with a theatrical bow that made her giggle. They followed their elder siblings onto the dance floor, but their performance couldn’t have been more different. Despite their best efforts to maintain dignity, their energy was wild and boundless, impossible to conceal. The music wrapped around them like silk ribbons, but Adelaide felt something else threading through the melody. Something cold. Wrong.

Her steps faltered as evil prickled across her skin like frost. The sensation crawled up her spine, raising every hair on her arms. Impossible. Not here. Corruption shouldn’t be able to breach the capital’s divine protections.

“Everyone get down!” Rowena’s voice cracked like a whip across the ballroom.

In a heartbeat, Adelaide and her siblings shifted from dancers to defenders. The transformation was so swift it took the assembled nobles several seconds to process what was happening. Screams began to rise as people realized this wasn’t part of the performance.

How had corruption breached their carefully maintained barriers? The capital’s divine protections should have held against any Aedrean incursion. Kaelen vanished in a burst of holy fire, reappearing on the upper balustrade before charging through an open doorway toward the source of the disturbance. Adelaide followed, her silk skirts barely whispering against the marble as she leaped with inhuman grace.

She landed beside the nobleman from the garden, closer now than she’d been in the courtyard. Goodness, but he was handsome up close. Strong jaw, intelligent eyes, and an aura of controlled power that made her pulse quicken.

“Do not permit your heart such turbulence,” she offered him her most reassuring smile. “I give you my word as a lady of the Order, you shall remain quite safe beneath our protection.”

His answering nod was grave and measured, but her attention had already shifted outward where true horror awaited. Through the tall windows, she could see them. A seething horde of chitinous nightmares. Most of their Order rested safely at the Summit of Seasons, leaving only the four of them here, unarmed, and caught completely unaware.

Yet Adelaide raised her hands in supplication, and He answered.

Power surged through her like sunlight through spring water. From the very stones beneath their feet, mighty trees began to rise. Oak and elm twisting their massive trunks skyward, branches weaving together in living fortification that would shame the greatest mortal walls. Vines thick as a man’s arm wound between them, bristling with thorns that gleamed like silver knives.

But movement at the western edge caught her eye, making her heart stutter with sudden fear.

“Rowena! A breach to the west!”

Divine wind howled in response, and frost crackled across the tall windows with sounds like breaking bones. The corrupted creature’s death rattle was lost beneath the sharp crack of its chitinous shell shattering. Midnight-black blood splattered like ink across the frosted glass, and what remained of its carapace clattered to the floor in pieces.

“Who—?” Adelaide began, turning toward her companion.

“Eyes ahead, Your Verdancy,” came his measured response.

His voice, smooth and cultured, with the particular gruffness that marked northern nobility. A sword master? Here, in this moment of chaos?

“Indeed, the One True Light shines upon us,” she breathed a laugh.

A flash of wry understanding crossed his noble features, the curve of his mouth bearing that particular brand of aristocratic amusement she had come to know so well among the court’s more interesting members.

“How might I be of service to you, Your Verdancy?” he asked, as if they stood in a drawing room rather than the midst of battle. The sheer absurdity of maintaining proper etiquette while corruption itself clawed at their doors made Adelaide want to laugh.

“First, I must express my most profound gratitude.” They both looked up. Rowena hovered above them, haloed by moonlight streaming through the damaged windows. “You have sheltered our youngest member with admirable valour. You have my deepest thanks, sir.” He bowed with solemn grace as Rowena descended like a golden leaf on autumn wind, her eyes glowing as she surveyed the chaos beyond Adelaide’s living wall. “Pray tell, how numerous are our adversaries?”

“I do not know,” Adelaide shook her head. Through the windows, she could see flashes of holy fire painting the courtyard in brilliant orange and gold. “Though Brother Kaelen manages the greater portion of their assault quite admirably.”

Rowena’s gaze shifted to their noble ally, assessing him with the same careful attention she gave to battlefield conditions. “Might you lend aid to my dear sister? She cannot maintain both barrier and battle, much as her heart yearns to join the fray.”

“Indeed, I shall,” his voice reverberated through Adelaide in ways that had nothing to do with divine power and everything to do with the way he looked at her.

Rowena offered him another respectful nod before turning back to Adelaide. “Our reinforcements approach, dearest.”

Adelaide nodded, and raised a gloved hand. The branches parted like curtains, creating a narrow passage. “Do take care,” she mouthed as Rowena passed through.

“Always, sweet sister.” Rowena’s brilliant smile flashed once before she dissolved into wind and moonlight, rushing to join the battle beyond.

The nobleman drew nearer, and divine revelation caught Adelaide’s breath. His spirit blade4 gleamed with pale blue radiance, waves of frost rolling from the blessed steel like morning mist. But more remarkably, it pulsed with power in a way she’d never witnessed in all her years of service. The rhythm was familiar somehow, like recognizing a half-remembered song.

How peculiar indeed.

His steel-grey eyes met hers for the briefest moment, and something passed between them. Recognition, perhaps, of kindred souls called to serve the Light. The moment stretched, charged with possibility.

Well, propriety demanded introductions, even in such extraordinary circumstances.

Adelaide’s lips curved upward in genuine delight. “I am Adelaide Veridis, Archon Spring3,” she said, as cheerfully as if they’d met at afternoon tea. “It’s a pleasure to make your acquaintance, despite our rather… unconventional meeting.”

His eyebrows performed an utterly charming dance, first lifting like startled birds, then drawing together as his grey eyes drifted to her in a perplexed sidelong glance. She pressed her lips together to contain her laughter.

“I am Rurik Iskaldsson, Duke of Aurenfell.”

Adelaide’s heart stopped, then began beating double-time. Iskaldsson? The Duke of Aurenfell himself? The legendary Wolf of Iskald? The sword master whose name was whispered with equal parts fear and reverence throughout the northern territories?

“What a curious fate,” she breathed. “Is it the Radiant Festival2 that brings the northern duke to grace our imperial capital at such a moment?”

A soft sound reached her ears, barely a laugh, more like a quiet exhale of amusement. “I suppose one could say that.”

The skirmish, for one could hardly dignify such swift violence with the term “battle,” swirled around them as Adelaide maintained their sanctuary. She created careful openings in her barrier to allow the imperial knights passage to protect the royal family, while the Order’s remaining guardians stayed outside with Kaelen and Rowena to hold the greater assault at bay.

And through it all, not a single corrupted creature reached her position. Not one.

She caught glimpses of Duke Aurenfell’s blade work through the spaces between her trees. Frost painting the marble in patterns of deadly beauty. His movements were spare and precise, each strike finding its mark as though guided by unerring instinct. Only their immediate vicinity remained unstained by the black ichor that leaked from Aedrean wounds.

A shift in the divine current drew her attention beyond the barrier. The assault ceased as suddenly as a snuffed candle, leaving an almost ringing silence in its wake. With a gesture and a whispered prayer, Adelaide parted her living wall to admit Kaelen and Rowena. Both looked weary. Kaelen’s flame-touched cape flickered with exhaustion, and Rowena’s gown had lost much of its earlier lustre.

“It’s finished,” Kaelen announced, brushing ash from his shoulder and leaving sooty fingerprints on his silk jacket.
