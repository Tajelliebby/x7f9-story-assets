The Father’s gifts were magnificent beyond description, yet they remained His mortal vessels, and Adelaide felt the weight of channeling such power settle upon her spirit. With a grateful prayer she released her hold upon the barrier, allowing the blessed trees to return to their peaceful slumber beneath the marble stones.

She moved to the balcony’s edge and surveyed the damage below with a heavy heart. Black corruption stained the pristine marble like ink spilled upon parchment, and several of the ornamental statues bore fresh cracks from the violence of the battle.

Such a shame about the banquet, and all the servants’ hard work…

“Archon Spring.”

She met Rowena’s gaze. Her sister’s golden eyes held a quiet command that Adelaide answered with two quick nods. Rowena smoothed Adelaide’s tousled hair with sisterly affection before turning to address Rurik with perfect noble grace.

“Your swift intervention has placed us most deeply in your debt, my lord. The Order does not forget such kindness.”

Rurik acknowledged her words with the same precision that marked his swordplay, a mere inclination of his head, no wasted motion. His ice blade dissolved like morning frost as he looked toward the royal family, still gathered safely upon the upper dais. Adelaide followed his gaze. Emperor Ardentis whispered urgently with his advisors while Queen Elowen kept protective arms about both her children. Prince Cassius stood at attention, hand resting upon his ceremonial sword, while Princess Lyria peeked around her mother’s shoulder with wide, curious eyes. Thorne caught Adelaide’s eye and offered the barest nod, his signal that all was secure and the immediate danger passed. Only then did he begin escorting the royal family from the ballroom, their guards moving in perfect formation around them. Adelaide waited until the last whisper of their presence faded from her awareness before allowing herself to fully relax.

The night wind carried the acrid scent of corruption through the broken windows, and her fingers still tingled with remembered divine power. She had no desire to look too closely at the destruction she knew waited in the courtyard beyond.

They had work yet to do.

The scent of burnt corruption filled Rurik’s lungs as the Autumn Archon meticulously counted heads, her quill scratching against parchment. He moved with practised efficiency, guiding trembling nobles toward the exit while the Summer Archon’s holy flames consumed what remained of their fallen enemies. Then came a sound. Subtle. Barely perceptible.

Click.

Rurik froze. What was that?

Shaking off the unease, he continued to escort the nobles until he noticed Archon Spring’s distress. His eyes widened as he watched her kick off her cream heels and dart barefoot across the cold marble floor, her gaze frantic. Her eyes darted about, lips moving in whispered prayer.

Click.

Realization crystallized in her expression as her arm swept through the air. The decorative vines shattered against the stone. From the debris something emerged, a nightmare given form, its jagged limbs catching the light like broken glass. It landed heavily near the arched double doors, an unholy screech splitting the air. Its limbs clicked against the marble with an unsettling rhythm. Gasps and cries filled the ballroom. Ladies clutched at their elaborate gowns while gentlemen moved instinctively to shield them.

“Duke Iskaldsson!”

Her voice cut through the chaos like a blade. Rurik needed no further instruction. His sword materialized in his grip, ice crackling along its length as divine power coursed through his veins. Yet he dared not wield it. Not yet. The creature blocked the only exit, its bulk pressing against the cowering nobles, diamond-adorned fingers clutching at one another in terror.

“Everyone, I implore you, lower yourselves and proceed with utmost caution towards the exit.” The Keeper of the Turning Leaves’s voice carried above the din, unwavering and clear.

She and the Keeper of the Eternal Flame moved in tandem, shepherding the guests to safety. His fiery cape billowed as he guided a group of women and children, while she floated above, issuing crisp orders to the knights below.

Rurik inched forward, testing the creature’s reactions. Its writhing appendages, grotesque mockeries of eyes, narrowed. The monster lunged with unholy speed, but not at him. A noblewoman stood frozen in its path, her scream piercing the air as she stumbled backwards. Her fingers clutched his arm in panic. The unexpected touch sent ice through his veins that had nothing to do with his powers. His blade faltered, suspended mid-strike as revulsion crawled across his skin.

“Run!” Her Verdancy’s voice rang out.

Her vines surged forward, yet even Rurik could see they would not reach in time. The creature’s stinger arced through the air, its path clear as a blade descending for the kill. His breath hitch, then he saw it. One moment she stood far away. The next she was there, between the woman and death itself. Her arms spread wide, a living shield. A shimmering barrier flickered into existence, divine power pulsing through it.

Tink.

The sound was almost lost in the chaos, but the sight of the stinger piercing through her shoulder branded itself into Rurik’s memory. Her breath hitched, the smallest betrayal of pain, before she bit her lip.

“ADELAIDE!” The Summer Archon’s roar held the fury of hellfire itself. The Autumn Archon’s cry followed, sharp as judgment day. The very air transformed into their battlefield, heat and wind writhing together in righteous anger.

The lady turned, throat bobbing as she faced the barbed appendage protruding slightly below her joint. The creature recoiled, but she moved first. Her hand shot up, gripping the stinger. Her vines followed, divine bindings constricting the beast’s writhing limb. The Aedra shrieked, its cries echoing through the marble hall like the wails of the damned. Yet Archon Spring held fast, drawing the barbs through her own flesh. With a final, vicious yank, the stinger tore free of her body. Black blood sprayed, splattering across the marble floor and her dress.

Above them the deafening screech faded into nothingness. Summer’s flames descended like divine wrath and Autumn’s winds howled with celestial fury, tearing into the creature until its upper half disintegrated in an instant. The explosion of power obliterated the wall beside them, the force sending shockwaves through the room. Through it all Her Verdancy erected a barrier mere moments before annihilation, protecting them from judgment’s backwash. The room fell into silence so complete that one could hear the whisper of dust settling.

After a few breaths the vines retreated. Their saviour rested against a marble pillar and smiled at the rescued woman. “Are you quite alright?” Her voice was steady, despite the sacrifice written across her shoulder. The woman, pale as freshly fallen snow, offered a jerky, almost mechanical nod, her trembling hands clutching her silk skirts. “Mercifully spared,” the Archon Spring giggled.

Through her relief Rurik saw the slight sway in her stance. He stepped closer, gaze raking over her wound, and hesitantly parted his lips. “Your condition?”

“I am perfectly well,” she insisted, gracing them with a smile that rivalled the light of the chandeliers themselves.

“There’s nothing satisfactory about your condition.” The Summer Archon materialized at her side, healing flames already flickering at his fingertips. “Della, you’re bleeding like a gutted deer.”

“I assure you, I am quite well.” Her fingers curled and uncurled.

The Autumn Archon descended through shafts of fractured light, her sharp eyes taking in the devastation: shattered stone, splintered wood, the lingering scent of corruption. “That creature was no ordinary Aedra,” she observed, a rare note of anxiety threading through her voice.

“Indeed not.” Her Verdancy’s throat bobbed. “Though we must thank the Divine that no one was—”

Her strength betrayed her. Somehow her weight settled against Rurik. Only when the warmth of her blood began seeping through his formal attire did awareness crash upon him like icy northern waters. The Autumn Archon rushed forward, concern writ across her face. The Summer Archon turned his palm upward, and something heavy settled in Rurik’s chest.

“I… I apologize,” he managed as he surrendered her to the care of Summer.

The Keeper of the Turning Leaves’s gentle touch on his shoulder meant comfort, but what comfort could there be? Archon Spring lay wounded because the Duke of Aurenfell, the supposed protector of the realm, had allowed a woman’s touch to stay his blade. As they carried her away he stared down at his hands. Her blood remained warm upon his skin, not accusatory, but damning all the same.

The Keeper of the Glimmering Frost appeared not long after with the Captain of the Guard, his expression grave as winter. His words died mid-sentence when his gaze fell upon the wounded form of Archon Spring. He rushed to Archon Summer’s side, looking utterly stricken as he gathered his sister into his arms. As he cradled her head against his neck, he continued his hurried conference with the other Archons, his voice too low for Rurik’s ears to capture.

When his eyes eventually found Rurik’s across the ruined ballroom, he offered a respectful bow, despite the precious burden in his arms. Rurik returned the gesture with a stiff nod, the weight of the Archon’s respect making his jaw clench tighter with guilt. Had he known of Rurik’s failure, surely that respect would have frozen into contempt.

After ensuring the remaining guests were safely escorted from the premises, Rurik approached the Captain of the Imperial Guards.1 “I wish to assist in whatever capacity might prove useful,” he stated flatly.

The Captain regarded him with surprise. “Your Grace, surely you require rest after such an ordeal?”

Rest? As if sleep would offer sanctuary when his conscience stood ready to torment him with visions of Her Verdancy’s blood spilling across marble floors.

“No,” Rurik responded simply, offering a curt shake of his head.

With a reluctant nod the Captain granted him leave to join the troops patrolling the capital. As they moved through the cobblestone streets, Rurik could not help but notice the white triangular banners drifting from roof to roof. Many hung in tatters where the Aedra had passed, in a single line, it seemed.

“Your Grace?” the Captain’s voice pulled him back to the present.

Lowering his gaze, Rurik noted several members of the Order2 who had joined their search party, their emblems catching the torchlight.

“Let us continue along this path.”

The Captain nodded, and their footsteps echoed against the cobblestones as they proceeded toward the border. Hours passed in meticulous examination. Every gate, every wall, every possible point of ingress was scrutinized. Yet not a single sign of forced entry presented itself. A harsh breath passed his lips as hues of gold and pink painted the sky, with no concrete evidence in sight. This will not do. I must find something—

“Your Grace.”

Rurik turned at the address, one eyebrow rising before he could master his expression.

“Speak.”

The Captain stood at attention, his posture betraying the discomfort of his message. “By order of the Emperor himself, Your Grace,” he announced with quiet authority as another guard shifted nervously at his side. “You must rest.”

Ah. That must be his escort.

“Very well.”

Rurik surrendered with as much dignity as he could muster, allowing himself to be guided back to the Valhyris Spire. The Imperial Palace rose like a monument to divine favour, its golden spires catching the morning light like a crown of celestial fire. In different circumstances he might have found it beautiful. His chambers awaited, appointed with the finest luxuries Valestra could offer a visiting Duke. His earlier displeasure at being drawn from his beloved Aurenfell seemed a trivial concern now, overwhelmed by greater failures. He had been summoned for the Radiant Festival, meant to celebrate the Season Archons and the divine blessings they brought to their realm. But surely, after such a catastrophe, the festivities would be postponed.

The thought brought little comfort. What good was a delay when Archon Spring lay wounded because of his weakness? What healing could time offer his conscience?

The heavy door closed behind him with the finality of a tomb being sealed. He wasted no time in ridding himself of his formal attire, dropping the blood-stained coat to the floor with disgust. Layer by layer he stripped away the garments that bore witness to his shame, until nothing remained between his skin and the cool morning air. The bathing chamber beckoned, a masterpiece of marble and gold befitting an Emperor’s guest. The sunken tub, built directly into the polished floor, awaited like baptismal waters. He descended the shallow steps and submerged himself, watching as the clear water pinked from the blood that had dried upon his hands and chest. If it had been his own blood spilled in her defence, it would have been a more honourable stain. Resting his head against the tub’s edge, he gazed through the tall windows where the city stretched before him. Morning light washed over streets and spires, bringing life to the sleeping capital. Somewhere within those walls someone suffered for his hesitation.

“Forgive me,” he whispered to the empty room.

Silence burdened him as the sun set and rose again over many days.

Knock, knock, knock.

Rurik looked up from his book, the sound cutting through the peaceful solitude of his evening. Marking his place with his finger, he studied the gilded tome resting in his lap, a history of the Northern Campaigns, something far more enjoyable than whatever awaited him at the door. With a resigned sigh he rose from his chair on the balcony, the cool night air clinging to him as he stepped inside. The fourth knock never came. As he swung open the door a young servant nearly rapped his knuckles against his chest, his eyes widening with surprise.

“For you, Your Grace,” he stammered, extending an envelope bearing the imperial seal, a phoenix wreathed in flames. The wax gleamed blood-red in the lamplight.

“Thank you,” Rurik replied, accepting the missive with a curt nod before closing the door.

He retrieved a silver letter opener from his desk before returning to the balcony. As he broke the seal and unfolded the parchment, his eyes widened.

Madness. Pure madness.
