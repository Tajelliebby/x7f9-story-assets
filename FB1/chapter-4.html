Adelaide stirred against the soft morning light filtering through her chamber’s curtains. The warmth painted everything in honey and gold, though something felt wrong. Her limbs moved as though she swam through thick water.

“Your Verdancy!”

Three faces hovered above her bed. Maris clutched her apron with trembling hands, copper curls bouncing as tears threatened to spill. Cecily clasped her hands together, hazel eyes bright with relief. Elaine stood straighter, her icy blue gaze sharp with concern despite her composed manner.

“Praise the merciful Light, you have returned to us!” Maris’s words tumbled over one another.

“We were so worried, my lady.” Cecily’s voice cracked. “You have been unconscious for so long…”

“Pray, compose yourselves.” Elaine’s firm tone cut through their chatter. “The lady needs peace. Sleeping for three days is nothing to scoff at.”

Adelaide struggled to sit up. Fire shot through her shoulder. “Three days?” The words scraped her throat like dry leaves. She coughed, the sound harsh and unfamiliar.

Cecily rushed forward with a glass of cool water. “Here, my lady.”

Adelaide drank slowly, then looked between their worried faces. “What happened?”

Maris gripped her hand as if afraid she might disappear. “You were grievously wounded, my lady. Poisoned by the Aedra’s stinger while protecting others at the congratulatory banquet. We feared…” Her voice broke. “We thought we had lost you.”

The weight of their terror pressed against Adelaide’s chest. She squeezed Maris’s hand, forcing a smile that felt fragile as spun glass. “I am still here.”

Elaine stepped closer, adjusting the pillows with practised efficiency. “You gave us all quite a scare. How do you fare at present? Shall I arrange a modest repast? You have sustained yourself naught these past days.”

Adelaide nodded, though the movement made the room spin slightly. “Something light.”

Maris bounced forward, copper hair catching the sunlight. “I shall fetch broth right away, ma’am!” She vanished through the doorway.

Adelaide let her head fall back against the pillows, closing her eyes. The lavender scent that usually comforted her felt overwhelming now. Her thoughts moved like honey, slow and thick, as she tried to piece together fragments of memory.

The first dance. Something had been wrong. The sharp jolt of unease. Then the attack.

The heavy oak door creaked open. Maris entered with a silver-rimmed tray that trembled in her small hands. The bowl of broth steamed gently, but its scent seemed distant, muted.

Cecily helped Adelaide sit up properly, arranging pillows behind her back. “Here you are, my lady.” Maris set the tray across Adelaide’s lap with careful hands.

Adelaide wrapped her fingers around the porcelain spoon. Each sip went down slowly, the chicken and herbs tasting faded, as if the essence slipped away before it could settle upon her tongue. Her maids watched anxiously. Maris fidgeted, biting her lip and darting glances between Adelaide’s face and the tray.

Adelaide forced another smile. “Thank you, Maris.”

As she sipped, a curious sensation pulsed beneath her skin, an insidious rhythm that made her feel out of sync with herself. She turned her focus inward, channeling her energy and tracing the threads of power within. The dissonance was easy to find: a foreign wound buried deep, like a splinter she could not see but could not ignore. She made a low sound but kept eating until the door opened again.

“Our youngest always causes such worry.”

“Sister Rowena!” Adelaide’s face brightened.

Rowena settled on the bed’s edge, handing a white envelope to Elaine. The scent of rosemary drifted from it. “How do you feel now?”

“Stuffy.”

“Poison or a curse?” Rowena leaned closer.

“I am not certain.”

Rowena placed her hand upon Adelaide’s shoulder and Adelaide handed her dish off to Cecily. They both bowed their heads. Soft words became forceful rebukes. Adelaide prayed alongside her, eyes tightening as sharp pains lanced through her shoulder. Something loosened but fought against being undone. With a violent lurch the foreign thing was expelled. Adelaide lurched forward, warm substance covering the sheets beside her bed. The substance was murky grey. Rowena made a sound of triumph, rubbing Adelaide’s back. Invisible wounds knitted themselves together. The dissonance faded as energy flowed freely once more.

“Oh dear.” Adelaide wiped her mouth, then whispered soft prayers of thanks.

“Indeed, that was no ordinary Aedra.” Rowena’s brows knitted. “I shall clear the bathing chamber for you. Take all the time you need.”

“I shall manage that myself.” Adelaide grinned. “Fetch me a basin, Maris.”

Rowena took her leave. Adelaide cleaned the area as much as she could before setting the soiled sheets aside. One of her maids volunteered to wash them while Adelaide went to bathe. In the warm water she whispered words to the Maker between scrubs.

Go to the emperor.

Adelaide paused mid-scrub. “Could you repeat that, please?”

More clearly she heard: Go to the emperor.

“Very well.” She did not question divine instruction. She finished bathing and told her maids of her plans. Predictably, they floundered.

“But have you not just awakened?” Maris worried. “You should rest, ma’am.”

“It is not my own decision, so I must go.”

Understanding dawned upon their faces. They dressed her in a simple gown, fitting enough for the royal palace. Adelaide informed her siblings of her visit. Rowena nodded, saying He had already told her that Adelaide needed to do this. Adelaide left without the carriage. She walked through the streets, greeting priests and knights of the Order. The people welcomed her warmly. Children ran to her side.

“Archon Spring! Will you play with us?”

She smiled, though she needed to reach the palace. “Perhaps a quick game of tag?” They chased her through the market square, and she stayed just fast enough to keep ahead. Her laughter danced upon the wind. When the palace came into view she stopped. “I must go now, little ones.”

They waved goodbye. Adelaide jogged forward, then leaped onto a nearby rooftop. The rush of air through her hair felt like freedom. Each leap was seamless, as if the wind itself carried her toward the Valhyris Spire. She paused behind a chimney, heart racing from the simple joy of using her limbs freely.

If Rowena could see her now, she would receive quite the scolding.

Below, a vendor scratched his balding head in bewilderment, puzzled by something he could not place. Adelaide giggled and blew a playful kiss toward his stall. Her magic danced from her fingers, making the apples and pears shimmer with newfound lustre in the morning light.

Small blessings for the innocent.

The vendor blinked, shrugged, and returned to his work. Adelaide took off once more toward the imperial palace. Its white marble and obsidian towers rose in elegant lines that seemed to defy gravity, piercing the sky like sentinels. The Valhyris Spire shimmered against the backdrop, its presence commanding attention from the farthest reaches of the empire. She approached the building and went straight to the emperor’s office, unseen by those she wished to avoid. The guards in the hall greeted her with knowing smiles.

“Your Verdancy,” one bowed slightly. “What brings you here unannounced?”

“I seek an audience with the emperor.”

The guards exchanged glances. “The emperor is currently in a meeting, but we shall inform him of your arrival.”

The emperor’s aide stepped out at that moment. His eyes widened comically upon seeing Adelaide. “Your Eminence?” he stuttered.

“May I see the emperor?” She smiled.1

“Yes, of course!” He ushered her inside.

The emperor looked surprised but less so than his aide. “Should you be moving about so soon?”

“I needed to meet you, Your Imperial Majesty. I feel you have concerns for me as I have concerns for you.”

“If you have sought me out, then my concerns are of less importance than yours.” He sighed heavily. “You go first.”

“Tell me yours.”

“The Radiant Festival—”

“Oh, no question. It will be held.” She smiled.

“But?” he stuttered.

“It will not be postponed.”

The emperor blinked and offered a soft smile. “Your Eminence has spoken and I obey… Now for the heavier topic?”

“Wait.” She turned to the aide. “Distribute the missive now. The festival will proceed. Tell the nobles the Order requires their willing service this season.” The aide nodded and quickly began writing. Then she turned back to the emperor. She hesitated only briefly, her gaze steady. “There is something amiss within the capital, an unseen threat that I must investigate. And it appears there are forces at work to keep me from doing so.”

Ardentis’s expression darkened. He must think the same, since their victory banquet had been crashed by Aedra who should not have been able to enter the capital. “I wonder to what we owe this betrayal… Has the Maker given you a name?”

“No.” She shook her head. “He only told me to come here.”

“Do you have any inkling of where to begin?”

“Yes, actually. Now that I speak to you, the places of interest have become clear.” Adelaide’s eyes drifted to the aide.

He understood, gathering his papers. “I shall have these copied and sent out shortly.” He hurried from the room.

Adelaide listened to his retreating steps, then traced her ear from tip to lobe. With a flick of her finger the air in the room stilled. Ardentis’ breath hitched. Even after so many years, it still took getting used to, the way the Great Archons could dampen sound in a room.

Adelaide’s expression grew sober, though her eyes retained their determination. “I must request permission to leave the capital, Your Imperial Majesty.”

The emperor’s casual air vanished, replaced by grave seriousness. “Leave?” He leaned back, clearly troubled. As one of the Great Archons, Adelaide was bound by law to remain close to the capital except under explicit permission. “To what end?”

“The great rifts on the western edge of Riftspire Citadel in Risenholt, and north of Bearhook, beyond the Borderwall in Aurenfell.”

“Truly the biggest of the rifts…” The emperor rested his face upon his laced fingers, sighing heavily. “But I cannot send you to either of those regions without an explicit reason still.”

“That is true, but the Lord will provide—”

“Your Grace?” came a guard’s voice from beyond the door.

“I seek an audience with the Emperor,” came a familiar voice that made Adelaide’s pulse quicken unexpectedly.

Adelaide looked at the door, to the Emperor, back to the door, and beamed. Would you look at that? Divine Providence.

Adelaide approached the door as the guard replied, “The Emperor is currently engaged, but we shall inform him of your arrival.”

“Thank you,” came the measured response from beyond the threshold.

“It is the Duke!”

Adelaide opened the door with that familiar chirp in her voice. The sight that greeted her made her breath catch. The man who had haunted her thoughts since their last encounter stood before her, looking utterly composed despite the urgency she sensed beneath his controlled exterior. His grey eyes widened slightly, then again did that fascinating dance that she found herself studying.

“Your… Verdancy…” he said, his voice carrying that same careful formality she remembered.

“Please,” Adelaide grinned, swinging the door wider in invitation. “Enter.”
