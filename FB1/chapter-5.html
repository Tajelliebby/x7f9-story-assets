Rurik felt rather… off. He had arrived burning with righteous indignation. Three days. They meant to proceed with the Radiant Festival after a mere three days, exactly as originally planned, despite an Aedra attack within the palace walls. Despite one of the sacred Archons lying wounded. The city remained blissfully unaware of the attack. Was this ill-conceived persistence merely to maintain morale among the capital’s citizens? Such folly could not stand unchallenged… or so he had thought. But now he looked at Adelaide, who smiled at him with an impish light in those striking green eyes. Rurik noted how sun-warmed her skin appeared in the afternoon light. She had looked so much paler when she had bled against him.

“Do you not wish to see the emperor?” she blinked at him.

What was he thinking right now?

“Yes, thank you.” He lowered his head and followed her into the imperial sanctum.

Though smaller than the grand halls, the study surrendered nothing in imperial grandeur. Crystal chandeliers cast their light across ancient tomes and caught on the gilt edges of family portraits: generations of Valhedrans watching over them with painted eyes. Emperor Ardentis seated himself on a carved throne-like chair, his fingers drumming against the armrest in rhythm with the ticking grandfather clock that stood in the corner.

“And to what do I owe this visit, when you have not deigned to grace a single social gathering with your presence since the night of the celebratory ball?” His cousin arched a single imperial brow.

Rurik clasped his hands behind his back, feeling the smooth leather of his gloves strain against his knuckles as he fought to keep his tone measured. “The ball was by your imperial decree, Your Imperial Majesty. I attended, as was my duty and privilege.”

The corner of Ardentis’s mouth twitched, though whether in amusement or frustration, Rurik could not tell. “Such bare minimum adherence to duty does not excuse your thorough withdrawal from society,” he leaned forward. The imperial phoenix seal on his signet ring caught the afternoon light. “You are the Duke of Aurenfell, cousin, and such isolation ill becomes your station.”

His gaze shifted to Adelaide, and Rurik’s followed unbidden. She smiled at them both, then curtsied before excusing herself. The door closed behind her with a soft click that seemed to release something in Ardentis’s posture.

“Speak freely,” the Emperor said, his voice softening as he addressed Rurik not as a subject, but as family.

“I question the wisdom of proceeding with the festival,” Rurik stated plainly. “Are you certain it is prudent, given recent events?”

Ardentis exhaled slowly, his shoulders dropping a fraction beneath imperial robes. “It was a request from the youngest Archon herself,” he confessed. “It is not something I can easily deny, regardless of how deeply I worry for her wellbeing.” Seeing her vibrant smile, Rurik had feared as much. “Ah, since you’re here,” Ardentis perked up, his expression shifting to one Rurik knew all too well.

And here it comes.

“Rurik,” he continued, his voice softening with an intimacy that made Rurik’s jaw clench, “you stand as both Duke of Aurenfell and Northern Guardian. Your position demands more than mere competence. It requires stability, not solely for your lands but for every soul who calls them home. Should misfortune befall you, who then shall carry forward your legacy?”

Rurik maintained his carefully neutral expression, though he felt his fingers twitch against the small of his back. The scent of aging parchment and imperial seal wax filled his lungs as he drew a steadying breath. “Aurenfell has weathered centuries, Your Imperial Majesty. It shall endure, with or without my direct lineage to guide it.”

“Your lands cry out for stability, yet you offer them nothing but uncertainty by maintaining your solitary existence.” Ardentis’s expression hardened like clay in a kiln. “A family, Rurik… a family provides what no amount of soldiers or servants ever could. They are legacy incarnate, the very future made flesh. Children bring not merely joy, but strength. Consider my own dear…”

“I am not you, cousin.” Something about the way his brows softened made Rurik’s chest ache, but he would not falter. “Aurenfell does not require heirs to function. It requires strong leadership.”

Ardentis’s jaw tightened, and for a moment they stared at one another across a chasm wider than the Rift itself. Then, with a deep breath, he straightened. “I speak now not as the boy who once shared your childhood games, Rurik, but as the divinely appointed authority of these lands. I, Emperor Ardentis Valhedran III, do hereby issue this imperial decree: you, Rurik Iskaldsson, Duke of Aurenfell, shall take a bride before the year draws to its close, for the good and stability of the north and all its people.”

The words struck Rurik like a physical blow. Marriage? Now? He opened his mouth to protest, indignation rising in his throat…

“I can help with that, Your Imperial Majesty!”

The voice, impossibly bright and utterly audacious, shattered the moment. Rurik turned, simultaneously with the Emperor. Their expressions formed a perfect mirror of bewilderment. Hadn’t she left? Something flickered at the edge of his awareness, a feeling he could not quite identify.

“Your Verdancy…” Ardentis spoke slowly, balancing imperial authority and something remarkably close to amusement.

“I do apologize for interrupting, Your Imperial Majesty,” she chirped, that mischievous smile dancing across her lips. Her gaze flickered briefly to Rurik.

He cleared his throat, attempting to regain some semblance of ducal composure. His fingers instinctively sought the comfort of his sword hilt, though he wore only the ceremonial blade in these halls. “Your Eminence,” he acknowledged, his voice a deep rumble edged with restrained irritation, “this is hardly a matter…”

“I can help!” she interrupted again, stepping forward with a confidence that bordered on reckless. The light from the window caught her golden hair. Tiny flowers woven through her curls seemed to bloom more vibrantly with her excitement. “You need a bride, Duke Iskaldsson,” she continued, “and I happen to be… well, available.”

The pause stretched tight. What did she just say? Surely he had misheard.

The Emperor’s gaze flickered between them, resting on Adelaide. His fingers stilled on the armrest, and the incessant tapping finally ceased. “You… what did you say?” His words emerged soft, scarcely believing the audacity of the moment.

“You cannot mean this in earnest,” Rurik declared, his voice a low rumble that seemed to vibrate through the marble halls. He took a step back. His boot heel struck the intricate mosaic floor with more force than intended.

Those eyes, incredibly clear, crinkled at the corners. “I assure you, I do,” she replied, adjusting the vine-patterned sleeve of her gown with casual grace. “You are unwed, as am I. Our union would only serve to strengthen the bond between the Empire and Aurenfell.”

Emperor Ardentis reclined. “Your Verdancy,” he intoned, a note of paternal caution threading his words, “this is not a decision to be taken lightly. You have only just recovered…”

“I am fully capable of making this choice.” Her words were clipped. The petals in her hair seemed to stand at attention. Her eyes did not waver from Rurik’s.

Something filled his chest, a peculiar mixture of amusement and something entirely foreign. But he had no time to dwell on this unfamiliar sensation. The situation demanded serious consideration, not frivolous contemplation.

“You would wed me so readily, then?” Rurik narrowed his eyes, studying her with newfound intensity. His hand unconsciously traced the wolf pattern embroidered on his sleeve. “Merely for political advantage?”

Adelaide hummed thoughtfully, averting her gaze toward the window where the imperial gardens bloomed. A small butterfly had perched on the sill, its wings opening and closing in perfect time with her contemplative silence.

“I would wed you to safeguard that which is vital to us both: your people and mine.” She met his eyes once again, and in that moment Rurik felt as though he stood before the altar of Iltheris himself. “This is far more than a political manoeuvre. It is a duty I am prepared to undertake.”

Her words silenced him. They were spoken with such conviction that he found himself questioning whether this audacious offer truly carried no ulterior motive… or if it mattered at all.

“Your Imperial Majesty?” Rurik turned to Emperor Ardentis, seeking guidance. No, seeking salvation from this unexpected turn of events.

The Emperor, who had been observing their exchange with the quiet amusement of one who knows far more than he reveals, allowed a soft chuckle to escape. “It would appear, cousin,” he said with remarkable nonchalance, “that the question of your marriage has resolved itself.”

Rurik’s jaw tightened until he could feel the pressure in his temples. After a measured breath, he turned back to Adelaide, whose green eyes smiled expectantly. “I would not compel you,” his voice lowered to a quiet timbre as he absently adjusted the cuff of his sleeve, “but if you believe this serves our mutual interest, then… I shall not refuse your offer.”

A laugh bubbled out of her, pure and clear as a mountain spring. Her hands folded neatly in front of her as she nodded. A tiny blossom from her hair drifted down to rest on the mosaic floor between them. “Then it is settled,” she said, turning back to the emperor. “Shall we proceed, Your Imperial Majesty?”

Emperor Ardentis watched their exchange with barely concealed delight, his fingers steepled before him like the spires of the Summit of Seasons. “Fortune has woven its threads most cleverly today,” he proclaimed. “I shall see to the necessary announcements.”

“Splendid!” Adelaide’s voice rang out, bright, almost musical. She moved over to him, keeping the distance between them friendly but deliberately crossing the invisible boundary. The scent of spring flowers followed her movement. “Will you be my escort to the festival this Saturday?”

“I… yes.” The words left Rurik’s mouth before proper consideration, an uncharacteristic lapse in judgement that he immediately regretted. He could scarcely believe he had accepted so easily. This Archon possessed a particular talent for blindsiding people, himself included.

She beamed brighter if possible. “Thank you kindly,” she said, her formal words at odds with the mischievous gleam in her eyes. Adelaide glanced toward the window as the temple bells chimed the hour. “In that case,” she said, folding one arm behind her back and extending an open palm to him, “we don’t have much time before the boutiques close.” Rurik blinked at her stance, and her smile grew wider. “Too much?” she asked, reading his expression all too well.

“Yes…” His brows knitted together of their own accord.

She burst into laughter. The light caught the gold flecks in her eyes as she turned to the Emperor and sank into a graceful curtsy. “We will take our leave, Your Imperial Majesty.”

As they departed, Rurik fell into step behind her, his gaze drawn to her attire almost against his will. She wore a surprisingly simple dress of pastel pink with an empire waist, its sheer sleeves and outer layer catching the afternoon light through the palace windows. He half expected it to transform the way it had during the banquet.

“Your Grace?” she called, her voice pulling him from his reverie.

“Yes?”

“Is there any style that you prefer?” She glanced over her shoulder, her golden curls bouncing with the movement. “If we’re to match, I’d like your input.”

Match?! His eyes widened, though she could not see his alarm as she faced forward again, leading them down the sweeping marble staircase. He touched his pounding temples, willing the throbbing to subside. “I am…” He licked his lips, choosing his words carefully. “I am uneducated in the styles of the capital. I shall defer to your capabilities.”

“Oh, I haven't the slightest idea,” she laughed, the sound light and unburdened. “I’ve spent far too long on the front line. I simply wear what they prepare for me.”

He felt his lip quirk upward despite himself. There was something disarming about her honesty, so at odds with the practised pleasantries of court life.
