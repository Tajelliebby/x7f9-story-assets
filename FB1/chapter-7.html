Her words struck him anew. This woman, this Spring Archon with her flower-threaded hair and disarming smile, would be his wife, his duchess, the mistress of Aurenfell.

Rurik bowed his head, studying the weathered leather of his gloves as they lay folded upon his lap. “Your Verdancy, I must beg your forgiveness for the night of the invasion,” he said, each word scraping his throat. “You would not have sustained injury had I acted with greater resolve. I watched you fall when the Aedra’s shadow touched you.” He forced himself to meet her gaze, to face whatever judgment she might render. “You have not yet recovered fully, have you?”

Adelaide merely smiled. “Your Verdancy is too much. Call me Adelaide.”

Other words withered upon his tongue, alarm rising in its place. “I would not…”

“A-de-lai-de,” she enunciated with a smile.

Rurik sighed and conceded, already recognizing an arduous campaign for which he was quite unprepared. “Lady Adelaide.”

She beamed and he swallowed all other complaints. Such was her peculiar power: to silence with nothing more than the curve of her lips. Their carriage rattled over cobblestones, passing beneath the shadow of the Summit of Seasons. Its marble spires reached heavenward, gleaming gold and silver in the afternoon sun, as grand as any cathedral he had read of in the old histories.

“Why do we not journey there?” Rurik frowned, glancing between the magnificent edifice and the woman seated across from him. “Is that not your residence?”

Adelaide’s serene countenance darkened. “Who dwells in a temple save the Deity Himself?” she replied. “Those spires are His house. We live behind them, with the rest of the Order, as do all His servants.”

Confusion settled over him. In all his years of study, in all the histories and protocols his father had beaten into him, the Archons were said to keep the Summit’s spires as their seat. The notion that the divine vessels of divine power lived among common guardians rather than in consecrated halls seemed almost heretical.

Noting his bewilderment, Adelaide softened. “Do not mistake me, Your Grace. The Light pervades all places at all moments, yet only He may inhabit a temple. We are merely vessels…” She paused, her gaze drifting toward the Summit’s gleaming spires. “…though it was not always so. Once, the Archons kept their vigil beneath those very arches.”

Curiosity stirred within Rurik at the shadow that crossed her countenance, but he did not press. Some doors, once opened, could never be properly closed again, and they had crossed enough thresholds for one day.

The carriage wheels groaned as they turned down a quieter street and rounded the great temple’s flank. Behind the spires, joined to them like a servants’ wing to a grand manor, stood a low, single-storey building of modest stone, arranged around a wide courtyard. To call it humble would be to grant it undue grandeur. It was functional and unadorned, with little to mark it as the dwelling of the empire’s most revered individuals save a banner bearing the Order’s emblem that snapped in the wind above the entrance. In the courtyard, men and women in plain garments moved with easy familiarity. Several practised sword play or drawing bows. Others hunched over manuscripts or attended to mundane tasks.

“Ah!” Adelaide suddenly exclaimed, nearly pressing her face to the carriage window.

Following her gaze, Rurik spotted the other Archons standing apart from their brethren. Like those they led, they wore simple attire, distinguished only by outer tunics that marked their station, garments that would not have been out of place at a country fair, let alone the imperial court. The carriage lurched to its final rest, and Adelaide reached for the door with characteristic eagerness. He intercepted her motion, some half-forgotten lesson in propriety surfacing from the depths of his northern upbringing.

“Wait… please,” the words came out more command than request. He softened immediately, conscious of how she had yielded to his previous dictates. “Allow me.”

She settled back against the cushioned seat, a curious expression flickering across her features, as though she were cataloguing yet another of his peculiarities for future reference. And perhaps she was. Rurik descended first, relishing the solid ground beneath his boots after the swaying carriage journey. Turning, he extended his hand to her. His hand trembled slightly, and he fixed his gaze on her face to mask his discomfort. She glanced from his eyes to his outstretched palm, a question forming in the slight furrow between her brows.

Before she could accept or decline, a shout split the air. “Della!”

A man with hair like crimson flame surged toward them, moving with all the subtlety of a cavalry charge. Without ceremony, he reached past Rurik and bodily lifted Adelaide from the carriage, crushing her against his chest. She erupted in giggles, returning his embrace with equal fervour.

“Brother Kaelen, where are your manners?” Following the voice, the remaining Archons approached at a more dignified pace. The Autumn Archon’s smile belied any genuine reproach.

She and Thorne offered Rurik formal bows, which he returned with appropriate depth, grateful for this return to protocol. Kaelen continued to clutch Adelaide as though she were a child rather than a priestess who had called forth vines thick as a man’s arm to impale Aedrean shadows. Yet he managed to lower himself in an approximation of a bow.

“Thank you for safeguarding our youngest once more,” Kaelen said, his amber eyes assessing Rurik.

Rurik bowed in response, his spine rigid. “It was my duty,” he replied, the words feeling hollow yet somehow safer than any alternative that sprang to mind.

Adelaide looked up at her siblings, and something passed between the four of them, a silent communion that excluded him entirely. Rurik watched their expressions shift in unison, confusion giving way to understanding and then to varied reactions. Kaelen’s arm around Adelaide tightened perceptibly, fury kindling beneath his skin like embers catching flame. The veins in his neck stood out sharply against his sun-bronzed skin. Adelaide patted his arm with quick, soothing motions.

“Ah, yes,” Adelaide said, motioning toward Rurik with the casual air of one who had nearly forgotten an unimportant detail. “This is Rurik Iskaldsson, Duke of Aurenfell.”

Thorne’s face brightened with unexpected kinship. “Oh? We have another northerner among us?”

“He’s my intended,” Adelaide announced.

“What?!” The Autumn and Summer Archons’ voices rose in unified shock while Thorne’s jaw dropped as though it had suddenly lost all connection to his skull.

The Winter Archon recovered first, raising a pale hand. “Wait, wait,” he said, authority tempering his astonishment. “Let us remove to more private chambers.”

Rurik’s heart, which had maintained a steady rhythm through blizzards and battles alike, quickened its pace. “Yes,” he heard himself saying, the word emerging as both acquiescence and surrender. “Perhaps that would be most prudent.”

He was to be judged, he realized, not by emperors or courtiers who understood the pragmatic necessities of political marriages, but by these three guardians who clearly viewed Adelaide as something infinitely more precious than a mere piece upon the imperial game board. How had he, Rurik Iskaldsson, Duke of Aurenfell, found himself betrothed to the Spring Archon? Clearly cherished by siblings who now regarded him as though he were an Aedra materialized before them… And the thought that troubled him most as he followed them toward their dwelling was not their potential disapproval, but rather why their opinion should matter to him at all.

Kaelen reluctantly returned Adelaide to her feet, and they proceeded into the sanctuary. Rurik observed with fascination how Adelaide was greeted by each member of the Order who noticed her return. The interior, though modest, held a dignity that surpassed many a noble manor. Clean-swept floors, well-worn furniture and walls hung with simple cloth panels depicting the four seasons. His fingers brushed against a wooden pillar as they passed, finding it smooth from countless touches, a place well used and well loved.

They entered a chamber with four desks arranged in perfect symmetry. Thorne positioned himself behind the northernmost one, his pale fingers gripping its edge as the Summer and Autumn Archons flanked him like loyal lieutenants. Adelaide remained at Rurik’s side, and when he glanced down at her, she offered him a smile that momentarily eased the vice around his heart.

“So to be absolutely clear,” Thorne began, enunciating each word, “you are to be wed?”

“Yes,” she confirmed, grinning.

Rurik’s newfound comfort utterly evaporated, and his stomach twisted upon itself like a serpent in pain. The silence that followed hung thick as incense in a sealed chamber. Almighty Light have mercy.

“No. Absolutely not.” Kaelen shattered it first, his temper flaring like a torch suddenly uncovered. “Allow me to speak with you…”

“No, Brother Kaelen,” Adelaide interrupted, her tone light yet edged. “You shall not intimidate my future husband. His Imperial Majesty has already bestowed his blessing upon our union. It is done.”

Kaelen looked as though he might ignite where he stood, his skin flushed the colour of forge-heated metal. The Autumn Archon’s composed demeanour fractured, her mouth opening and closing in silent protest like a fish out of water. Thorne, however, appeared lost in contemplation, concern etching lines across his brow.

“Adelaide,” he said slowly, measuring each word, “pray, remind me. What precisely is your age?” His ice-blue eyes narrowed with calculation.

“I shall turn five-and-twenty next spring,” she answered with a careless shrug.

“Five-and-twenty?” Rurik’s head snapped toward her with such force he nearly strained his neck. The Light preserve him, he had assumed her older.

Adelaide giggled at his evident dismay. Thorne pressed his palm against his eyes as though warding off pain. “Adelaide, he is nine years your senior…”

She blinked at him, her brows rising slightly. “Oh? Is that so?” That revelation proved too much for the Autumn Archon, who swayed. The sound of her fall was barely audible, elegant even in unconsciousness. “Sister Rowena!” Adelaide gasped, rushing to kneel beside her fallen sibling. “Wake up! Do not faint away! I have need of your counsel!” She frantically fanned Rowena’s face with a parchment snatched from the nearest desk.

This was ridiculous.

Rurik remained rooted to the spot, feeling light-headed himself. The Light was testing him sorely this day, and he feared he might be found wanting.

Kaelen had calmed somewhat, perhaps finding satisfaction in Rurik’s evident discomfort. “You will relate the full account,” he commanded, his tone brooking no refusal.

“I, ah…” Rurik struggled to compose himself, feeling like a raw recruit facing his first field inspection. “His Imperial Majesty presented me with a decree to take a wife forthwith. Her Eminence… offered herself as my bride.” He swallowed hard. “I must apologize, I could not dissuade her from this course.”

“No, it’s quite all right. We understand,” Thorne said, rubbing Kaelen’s shoulder, though the Summer Archon’s expression suggested he was contemplating thoughts that bordered dangerously on treason.

“Ah, yes,” Adelaide perked up. “He’ll accompany me to the festival on Saturday.”

Her brothers exchanged a loaded glance. Kaelen’s gaze drifted to the ceiling, a vein in his temple becoming more pronounced. “How nice,” Thorne chuckled softly. “You have always wished to attend with a companion.”

“Yes!”

Rurik felt as though her enthusiasm mocked his discomfort. He cleared his throat. “I will be dressed appropriately for the occasion. Her Eminence saw to it.”

The eldest chuckled again. “Of course she did. And there’s no need for you to be so stiff. We’ll all be family soon.”

“Yes, well,” Rurik stammered. More than twice this afternoon his thoughts had been unravelled. Was he truly so transparent? “I do not wish to overstay my visit. I should return to my lodging now.”

Thorne moved closer and touched a single finger to his forehead. Warmth blossomed through Rurik’s body as he spoke. “May the Light go with you,” he pronounced. “Go in peace.”

Rurik offered a low bow, more deeply than protocol demanded, and took his leave. Familiar faces saluted him in the corridor, soldiers and courtiers alike, and he returned their greetings with a slight lowering of his head.

Settled in the carriage, his mind wandered to Adelaide.

It wasn’t merely her boldness, though that alone was both infuriating and ludicrous. Was it her gaze? Those eyes. Contradictory. Revealing everything and nothing in a single glance. Not the simpering gaze of court beauties, nor the calculated look of social climbers. No, her regard was something entirely foreign. And this farce. What could she possibly hope to gain from a marriage to a man like himself? She said she did it out of duty, but it seemed only a half-truth. She wanted something. He was certain of it. Not him, precisely, but something just beyond his comprehension.

And that unnerved him more than anything else.
