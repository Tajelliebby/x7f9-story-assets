The backstage area of Stellarbrook Amphitheater smelled like stale coffee and anticipation. Keanu took a drag of his cigarette, shifting the platinum strands from his face as he exhaled into the crisp February air. The metallic tang of stage fog mixed with the salt breeze rolling in from the Pacific, a taste he'd known for half his life.

Six PM felt like a lifetime away.

He slouched against a metal folding chair, combat boots crossed at the ankles, watching the roadies test cables and adjust monitors while tech crew move like ants across the distant stage. He took another drag, the cigarette burned down between his fingers.

"Man, you're gonna kick the bucket at this rate."

Callum's voice cut through his thoughts. The frontman waved a hand through the gray cloud, long dark hair shifting around his face in loose waves, , green eyes already bright with that pre-show energy Keanu used to feel. Used to.

Keanu took another drag. "A&L covers it, right?" The laugh that escaped held no humor. "Hell of a health care coverage."

"Boss woman would throttle you for that." Callum dropped into the chair beside him with a creak of metal, scratching at his stubble.

They sat in silence, the kind they'd perfected over fourteen years of friendship. Around them, the crew moved around. Adjusting mic stands, taping down cables, building the temporary world where Black Vortex would exist for one more night.

The mandatory hiatus had been A&L's call. Rest, Lars had said, like Keanu was a machine that just needed maintenance. Maybe he was. Maybe that's all any of them were. Instruments that needed tuning, voices that needed preservation. Well, at least A&L gave a shit. At seventeen, signing with Horizon Sound, they'd been too stupid to read the fine print. At nineteen, they got out. Moving labels had been the best decision they'd made as dumbass teens.

A grin spread across Callum's face, flashing pointed canines. "Feels crazy to be on a stage again after what? Two years?"

"A year, seven months, and two weeks." He still wasn't sure the opening gig was worth crawling out of hibernation for.

Callum froze, he sat forward with knitted brows. "You need to get out more."

"I'm good." Keanu reached for the pack in his jacket pocket. Callum's hand shot out, snatching the cigarette before it reached his lips. Keanu dragged a hand through his split hair, the platinum side catching the harsh backstage lighting. "Brother."

"Don't brother me."

Keanu stared at the stolen cigarette between Callum's fingers.

"Keanu. Callum."

Charlotte's voice carried the particular edge that meant business. Their manager appeared from the wings, military posture, black hair threaded with silver catching the stage lights. She looked at Keanu's empty hands, then at Callum holding the confiscated cigarette, and her expression remained unchanged.

"Can you at least pretend to care?" she asked.

"He cares," Callum said. "He's just dead inside."

"Accurate," Keanu muttered.

Charlotte's mouth twitched, the closest she came to a smile. "Sound check in ten. Midday Signature wants to meet their seniors first."

The word seniors settled oddly. Five years wasn't that much of a gap, but in this industry, it might as well be a generation.

Callum strode forward, hand extended toward their lead singer. "Adonis, mate." The handshake turned into a pull, a pat on the shoulder. "First concert. Full house too. You guys deserve it."

Adonis's blonde curls caught the stage lights, black-painted nails gleaming as he gripped Callum's arm. "No, thank you for opening. Seriously! Having you guys here means everything."

The rest of Midday Signature clustered around them. Raven's asymmetrical bob swung as she leaned in, grinning. "We grew up on your stuff. This is surreal."

Keanu hung back, bass already strapped across his shoulder. Leo, their drummer, caught his eye and nodded. Keanu felt his mouth curve, barely, but enough. These kids had worked their asses off. Let them have this.

"Am I late?"

The voice cut through the group's conversation. Keanu's gaze lifted. Pink hair. That was the first thing he registered. Bubblegum pink cascading in waves past delicate shoulders, a color so aggressively cheerful it made his teeth ache. He studied her for a moment, trying to place her. Not uncommon for A&L artists to support each other at shows, but he'd never seen this one before. Probably not on their studio floor.

Raven perked up immediately, pulling the girl into a hug. "Eden! You're here!"

Eden. The name meant nothing to him.

Before Keanu could process that, Callum threw an arm over the pink-haired girl's shoulder, making her stumble with an "oof." He nuzzled her hair in that big-brother way that usually annoyed the shit out of Keanu, but she just laughed. "You're just in time." Callum's grin was wide as he turned to the group. "Guys, this is Eden. She'll be performing Executive Lullaby with us!"

Keanu's fingers stilled on his bass. The words hit like a missed chord.

Keanu's eyes widened. His gaze snapped to Callum, searching for the punchline, but everyone else was nodding like this was common knowledge. Where the hell had he been when this decision was made?

The girl, Eden, was looking at him now. Hazel eyes that shifted between green and gold, bright with something he couldn't name. He narrowed his eyes, and she quickly looked away.

What the hell was she staring at?

His jaw clenched. At least "Executive Lullaby" didn't have any growling. Lucky for her. He wasn't exactly in a singing mood.

Not right now, anyway.

"Play nice, Jace," Charlotte murmured beside him, her voice pitched for his ears only.

"I'm always nice."

The girl was already bouncing on her toes, all perky energy and bright enthusiasm. Keanu looked toward the exit, wondering if he should finish his smoke. Then he heard a soft "Excuse me."

He turned. She stood there, hand extended, hazel eyes bright. Up close, he could see the delicate beauty mark below her left eye. "Jace, right? I'm Eden."

"Call me Keanu."

Jace had been dead for a long time.

"Okay." She smiled, still waiting. He realized he should probably shake her hand. Her grip was firmer than he expected.

"Good luck out there," he said finally, the words awkward in his mouth.

Her smile brightened like he'd given her something precious. Was this some new kind of attack? He braced for something but she bounced on her feet again.

"Thank you!" thanked him and skittered to Callum.

Why the hell was she beaming at him like that?

Keanu shook his head and joined the rest of the group, hooking up his bass and checking his mic. The sound techs ran their tests. Levels were good. Everything was running smooth, efficient, the way A&L always operated. He adjusted the strap on his shoulder, rolled his neck, and tried to settle into the familiar pre-show rhythm.

"Alright, you bastards!" Callum laughed. "Time to show these festival rats what real music sounds like." His green eyes sparked with that familiar pre-show intensity, the kind that had gotten them through countless gigs since high school. "You ready for this, mate?"

"Always," Knox grinned, twirling a drumstick between tattooed fingers.

Beckett cracked his knuckles, red hair falling into his eyes. "Ready to melt some faces."

Kenji adjusted his glasses, nodding silently from behind his keyboard setup.

Keanu shrugged, leather jacket creaking with the movement. "As ready as I'll ever be for a pop princess to butcher our song."

Eden laughed, light and unbothered. "Don't worry. I'll do my best not to embarrass you."

"Ready? Just like we practiced." Callum turned to Eden.

Keanu dropped his head to check the pedals but snapped back so fast he could've pulled something.

Eden's voice cut through the air. Raw, powerful, the kind of fry scream that could wreck vocal cords if you didn't know what you were doing. But she knew. The technique was there, underneath the emotion, supporting that gorgeous, gritty sound that had no business coming from someone who looked like a strawberry shortcake.

When was the last time he'd heard a note like that?

He looked at Callum, expecting his friend to maybe---he didn't know---stop her before she damaged something. But the bastard was over there whistling, doing absolutely nothing.

"You heard this shit, brother?" Callum called over the mic.

"Sounds good." He offered a curt nod, not sure what the hell Callum wanted from him.

"Damn right. Glad the label let me take you on stage." Callum nudged Eden, and Keanu had to grit his teeth.

Of course it was Callum's idea. Of course. That idiot might get him before lung cancer did. He watched from the side as Knox caught Kenji's attention, and Kenji elbowed Beckett. They were all in on this. He was too old for this shit.

After a forty-five minute break, excited chatter started to fill the auditorim.

"Showtime, people!" A festival coordinator appeared in the doorway, clipboard in hand and urgency in his voice. "Black Vortex, you're up in two!"

Callum gathered them in their traditional circle. "Right, here's the deal. We go out there, we give them everything we've got, and we remind them why they fell in love with rock and roll in the first place." He turned to Eden, who had been hovering at the edge of their group. "You ready for this, love?"

She nodded, hazel eyes crinkling. "Ready."

"Good." Callum's grin was sharp. "Let's go make some noise."

Keanu glanced at Eden as she hung back, letting them take the lead. The moment they stepped into view, the crowd's energy hit like a wall of sound. Thousands of voices already chanting before they'd even reached their positions.

"Axe! Axe! Axe!"

Callum's stage name thundered from the crowd. He stepped into the spotlight, his signature fry scream tearing through the air. The crowd went absolutely insane, a sea of bodies surging forward against the barriers. The familiar rush of adrenaline coursed through Keanu's veins as he took his position at the back. This was what he lived for---this moment when the music became bigger than all of them.

"There's someone I'd like to introduce to the stage," Callum leaned into the mic, his voice cutting through the festival noise. "Give it up for RIN!"

To Keanu's surprise, the crowd went absolutely mental when she practically pranced onto the stage. The pink hair that had seemed so out of place backstage now looked deliberate, striking against the festival lights. She raised her hand to the crowd, and they screamed back their approval. Signs appeared from nowhere, and the energy shifted into something electric.

"Great to have you here, kid," Callum said, his grin visible even from Keanu's position.

Keanu felt his jaw clench.

"I'm glad to be here!" Eden's voice carried over the crowd with surprising strength.

Callum's voice dropped to that dangerous rumble that made crowds lose their minds. "We lay down, but only you sleep sweetly!"

They cued up for the fan favorite "Executive Lullaby." And it was---well, after almost two years of being on hiatus, he felt alive. The roar of the crowd and his heart competing for dominance in his chest. He actually felt thirty-two again instead of ancient. And that voice. He felt his lip twitch watching her keep up with Callum's vocal intensity. This wasn't some pop princess trying to sing rock. This was a rock singer who happened to have pink hair. Who was her senior? Mei-Ling?

No, seriously. Who was this kid?

The song ended too soon. Eden gave Callum a deep bow, then turned to the audience with that genuine smile, waving as she left the stage. They rolled into another fan favorite, "Lipstick on the Guillotine." Keanu's growl rumbled through the amphitheater, that familiar sound that made the crowd scream back. He lost himself in it. For forty-five minutes, he wasn't Keanu the burnout on mandatory rest. He was just the music, just the moment, just alive.

The lights were blinding. The heat was oppressive. The noise was chaos. Sweat clung to his forehead, his split hair sticking to his skin, but he didn't care.

It was perfect.

When they finally rolled off stage and got the cue that their mics were off, his ears were ringing and his shirt was soaked through. The adrenaline still coursed through his veins, making his hands shake slightly as he set down his bass.

Callum appeared at his elbow, two bottles of water in hand. Sweat plastered his dark hair to his face, and his grin was wild with post-show energy.

Keanu took the offered water gratefully, draining half of it in one go. His voice came out rough. He threw an arm around Callum, both of them breathing heavy, hair plastered to skin. "You better not do that again." Keanu's smile was tight. "I do not like going in blind."

Callum held up his hands in mock surrender, still grinning. "Oh my bad. Scout's honor I'll never do it again."

Keanu's expression flattened. "Get the hell outta here, man." He shoved Callum forward.

Callum's laugh echoed through the backstage area. Keanu watched his friend disappear into the crowd of crew and artists, then looked back at his bass, still sitting where he'd left it.

Well.

At least tonight wasn't all bad.
