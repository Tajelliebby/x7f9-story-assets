Keanu stared at the sky from his balcony, cigarette burning between his fingers. A week had passed since that exhilarating night at the amphitheater, and he was right back where he'd started, slouched against the railing, watching clouds roll in from the Pacific. He blew out smoke. Huh. Guess he wasn't the only one feeling stormy.

His phone buzzed against the metal railing. The caller ID read "Demon Lord." He considered ignoring it. Let it go to voicemail, pretend he hadn't seen it. But Lu would just call again. The man was relentless.

He answered. "I'm on hiatus."

"Come in." Lu's voice carried that particular brand of tired authority that made arguing pointless. "We need to talk."

"About?"

"A project. You're restless."

"I'm resting."

"Get your ass to the studio." The line went dead.

Keanu stared at his phone, then at the half-finished cigarette in his other hand. He could ignore it. Stay here, chain-smoke until the sun went down, order takeout he wouldn't finish. But his legs were already carrying him inside to grab his keys.

The ride to A&L Universal took fifteen minutes through Melody Mile's mid-afternoon traffic. Palm trees lined the boulevard, their fronds rustling in the salt-tinged breeze. The label's building rose ahead, twelve stories of glass and steel catching the California sun, musical note motifs etched subtly into the facade. Water features flanked the landscaped entrance, sculptures by local artists scattered across the plaza.

Keanu had been coming here for thirteen years. The building still looked too clean, too optimistic. The lobby stretched upward, all high ceilings and living walls, floor-to-ceiling windows showcasing gold and platinum records like trophies. A few artists lounged in the comfortable seating areas. He recognized most of them, nodded to a couple as he passed. The coffee bar in the corner was doing brisk business, the former musician who ran it calling out orders with the ease of someone who'd memorized everyone's preferences years ago.

Keanu jabbed the elevator button for the tenth floor. Executive territory.

The elevator ride felt longer than it should have. He checked his phone. 1:47 PM. They'd arranged to meet at 3 PM, but he'd gotten restless waiting in his loft, chain-smoking on his balcony until the urge to actually do something overrode his usual reluctance to show up early anywhere.

Not that he was excited or anything. He just had nothing better to do.

The doors opened. Executive floor. Polished hardwood, tasteful art on the walls, the kind of quiet that came from thick carpeting and closed doors. He headed for Lu's office, found Renee instead. She looked up from her desk, her elegant features softening into a smile. Then her nose wrinkled.

"Keanu." Her voice carried that particular warmth that would fool anyone who didn't know better. "Are you okay? You've been smoking again."

"Oh, I'm fine." He shoved his hands in his jacket pockets. "Resting."

Her brows knitted, soft concern radiating from her posture. Keanu wasn't buying it. This woman was just as ruthless as her husband, just better at hiding it. After a moment, her smile returned. "Lars is on the second floor. Studio D."

"Thanks."

He saw himself out of that office expeditiously.

The elevator ride down gave him time to wonder what the hell Lu wanted. A project. Right. Because mandatory rest meant nothing when the Demon Lord decided you were done resting. He nodded to some staff and artists as the doors opened on the second floor, then sauntered toward Studio D.

Through the glass, he could see Lu at the mixing desk, headphones around his neck, steel-gray hair catching the studio lights. The older man glanced back, pale blue eyes landing on Keanu.

"Give me a moment." The older man's attention returned to the booth.

Keanu nodded and dropped onto the couch behind the speakers, tucking his hands in his pockets as he settled into a man-spread. Through the glass, he could see two people in the recording booth. Nova, unmistakable with her cropped curls and the confidence that made her seem taller than 5'8" and---who was that?

"Okay, Cayo, Nova." Lu spoke into the microphone. "The intro, chorus, and first verse are solid. Let's take thirty."

An R&B and rap collab. That was a choice. One only Lu would call. No doubt about it. Cayo and Nova couldn't be more different. The smooth Spanish crooner and the alt-pop grunge rapper. But then, that's what made Lu legendary. He heard things other people didn't.

"Oh my god!" Nova's raspy voice filled the space as soon as she exited the booth, gray-blue eyes bright with mischief. She puffed out her chest, planting her hands on her hips like she was ready to challenge someone to a fight. "Look who crawled outta their cave," she cooed as if proud.

"Nice to see you too, Nova." Keanu snorted.

Cayo offered him a nod, and Keanu appreciated that the kid kept to himself. Though sometimes it was hard to remember this hulking six-foot-two mass was only twenty-two. Damn, what were they feeding him? "You alright?" The Spanish accent was strong on Cayo's words, warm hazel eyes checking him over with genuine concern.

"I'm good, kid."

"Yeah, uh-huh." That was Nova.

"I called him here." Lu walked past all of them, and they followed him out of the studio like ducklings. He nodded to Keanu. "Let's go for a walk."

Keanu stepped up beside him.

"Ooh, you in trouble," Nova called after them.

Keanu scoffed. She laughed.

They took the stairs instead of the elevator, Lu's long strides eating up the steps with practiced ease. The man was in his late fifties but moved like someone twenty years younger. Keanu kept pace, letting the silence settle between them. Lu would talk when he was ready.

"You're bored."

Well, that was quick. Keanu shrugged. "I'm on mandatory rest."

"Only two years," Lu's voice was flat, matter-of-fact. "You're already climbing the walls."

"...Maybe."

"I have a collaboration for you." Lu pushed through the stairwell door onto the first floor studio territory Keanu rarely used. Third floor was for bands and this level wasn't. "Something different. Shake things up."

"What you got for me?" Despite himself, Keanu felt a flicker of interest. "But not PHASEØ, those kids are... energetic."

Lu's mouth twitched. "Don't worry."

They walked the grounds, passing studios with artist-painted doors, each one unique. Keanu found himself smiling despite the wariness creeping up his spine. "You know, it's funny. You outbid Horizon for this building. It's so nice too."

Lu glanced at him, pale blue eyes glinting. "Let me tell you a secret. They wouldn't have been able to pay for it anyway. I did them a favor."

Oh, he is cold.

"I'm sure Chen hates to see you coming." Keanu smirked. "You could buy out Horizon, put them to rest."

Lu arched a brow, then chuckled, a rare sound. "More work than it's worth."

"I believe you." Keanu hummed.

Lu's smile turned sharper. "Although I may have made a joke or two about expansion."

"You're my kind of evil."

They stopped outside Studio B. The first floor layout was different from what Keanu was used to cleaner lines, newer equipment. Lu opened the door and music bled through, atmospheric and layered. Sly sat at the mixing desk, headphones around his neck, dark hair messy like he'd been running his hands through it. Beyond the glass, three girls.

The very Korean looking one was all perfect posture and sleek black hair pulled back like she was attending a business meeting instead of a recording session. The shorter one beside her bounced on her toes, trendy highlights catching the light as she swayed to the track. And there, center position, pink hair impossible to miss.

Eden.

She adjusted her headphones, hazel eyes focused on the perforated music stand in front of her.

"Eden, sustain that note. Again." Sly's voice came through the speakers.

Keanu watched her take a breath, shoulders rising. The track rolled. Then she sang, voice soaring over the production:

"We don't need to oversay
You've been here the whole way
Every message lands because
You listen like it's love"

That high note held, not falsetto, pure chest voice, for eight seconds while her groupmates wove the final chorus around her. Sly threw up a thumbs up, grinning. Lu nodded once, approving.

Keanu stood there wondering who the hell had sung at the concert a week ago. Because what he just heard was a pop star. Competent, sure. Range for days. But this wasn't the raw rock voice that had matched Callum note for note. This was manufactured polish.

"Sly." Lu's voice cut through. "Let me talk to Eden."

Sly glanced back, caught sight of them, and nodded. He spoke into the mic. "Eden, you got a sec?"

All three girls looked up. The dark haired one's expression remained neutral, assessing. The shorter one perked up immediately, waving. Lu returned the wave. Eden's smile came slower. She pulled off her headphones and slipped out of the booth, pink hair bouncing with each step.

"Keanu, this is Eden." Lu's voice stayed level. "Eden, this is Keanu."

"We met at the concert." She giggled.

Keanu focused on keeping his expression neutral. That giggle.

Lu paused. His pale eyes moved between them, then he snapped his fingers softly. "Oh. Right."

Is this what's waiting for me at fifty? Memory loss?

"I propose a collaboration." Lu continued like he'd never lost his train of thought, folding his arms across his chest.

Keanu kept his voice flat. "With the pop princess?"

"Am I introducing you to anyone else?" Lu didn't even blink. "Three-track single."

"Two."

"Hm... you two figure it out."

"And if I say no?"

Lu shrugged, silver hair catching the overhead lights. "Then you say no." He paused, then added, "She's not just some manufactured pop star. She's got real talent. You saw it yourself on stage."

Keanu had seen it. That was the problem. It would be easier to dismiss her if she was just another pretty face with auto-tuned vocals. But there had been something in her voice that night. The version standing in front of him now didn't match. Which one was real?

He met Lu's gaze. "No."

"Okay." Lu turned and walked away.

Just like that. No argument, no pressure, no guilt trip. The older man disappeared down the hallway, leaving Keanu standing there with Eden watching him, hazel eyes curious and unbothered.

Lars if you hate me just say it. He must have done something truly unforgivable in a past life.

"Sorry about him." Keanu shoved his hands in his pockets.

"It's fine." Eden's smile stayed in place, bright as ever.

He nodded once, awkward. "Uh, sorry for the interruption." He shuffled toward the exit.

What the hell was Cupid trying to do? Besides Keanu wasn't some starry-eyed kid ready to fall into the Demon Lord's matchmaking schemes. He had enough problems without adding a pop princess to the list.

He took his time riding back to his loft, enjoying the purr of his classic R18's engine. He parked then climbed the steps only when he at the landing he saw a car around the corner. Is that who he thinks it is?

The door swung open before he could turn the key. Callum stood there, green eyes wide with something between amusement and exasperation. "You said no?"

Keanu blinked. "Lu told you?"

"Charlotte did." Callum stepped aside, letting him in.

Of course. They were always conspiring against him. But the smell of fried chicken drifting from inside made him reconsider any homicidal thoughts. Fine. They could live.

His bandmates had made themselves at home. Beckett sprawled across the black leather couch, scrolling through his phone with a beer dangling from one hand. Knox was halfway through a bucket of wings, eating with the single-minded focus of someone who'd skipped lunch entirely. Kenji sat cross-legged on the floor by the coffee table, methodically working through coleslaw like it was a meditation practice.

"One day," Keanu said, grabbing a beer from the six-pack on his counter, "I'm changing those locks."

"You've been saying that for three years." Callum dropped back into one of the mismatched chairs. "So about that collab?"

Knox looked up, sauce on his fingers. "With the LUNARIA girl, yeah? The pink-haired one?"

LUNARIA? That's the group name? Ick.

"No." Keanu took a long drink.

Beckett finally glanced up from his phone, red hair falling into his eyes. "Brother, you turned down a collab. With a girl who can actually sing. What's the play here?"

"There's no play. I'm on mandatory rest."

"You're going out of your mind." Knox tossed a bone into the bucket. "We can all see it."

Callum leaned forward, elbows on his knees. His voice lost its usual teasing edge. "When's the last time you actually enjoyed making music? And don't give me some bullshit answer about the concert. I mean really enjoyed it."

Keanu stood there, beer halfway to his mouth. He glanced out the grid-framed windows, the sun was bleeding into the Pacific, turning the sky amber and gold. Then he put the bottle to his head.

"That's what I thought." Callum sat back. "Do the collab."

"You're playing mind games with me."

"Is it working?"

"...Fuck off."

But the words had no heat behind them. Callum knew it too, judging by the grin spreading across his face. They stayed until the food was gone and the beers were empty, sprawled across his living room like they'd done for fifteen years. The conversation drifted, new gear Knox wanted to try, a riff Beckett had been messing with, whether Kenji's latest keyboard setup was overkill or genius. Normal shit. Easy shit.

Eventually they filtered out. Beckett first, yawning and complaining about an early morning session. Knox and Kenji together, still debating synthesizer configurations. Callum lingered at the door, green eyes searching Keanu's face.

"Don't overthink it, mate," he said quietly. "Just... see what happens."

Then he was gone too, and Keanu was alone with the debris of their invasion. Empty bottles clustered on the coffee table. Napkins scattered across the wood surface. The lingering scent of fried chicken and the particular silence that followed other people's noise. He was climbing the open-riser stairs to the upper level when his phone rang.

Old Man.

Keanu smiled despite himself and answered. "Hey."

"TELL HIM HE'S BEING A DUMBASS!" Callum's voice erupted in the background, loud enough that Keanu had to pull the phone away from his ear.

"Is that---"

"He showed up at the shop ten minutes ago." Lucas's voice was warm, amused, the weathered rasp of someone who'd spent decades around engine grease and honest work. "Won't leave until I 'talk some sense into you,' apparently."

"I'm hanging up."

"No you're not." Lucas huffed. "Heard you got offered a project."

Keanu reached the loft sitting area, dropped onto the low-profile sofa. "Everyone's a damn snitch."

Lucas was laughing. "You gonna do it?"

Keanu dragged a hand through his hair, the platinum side catching what little light remained. "I don't know. She's... pop."

"So?" A pause, then Lucas continued, gentler. "You used to love experimenting. Pushing boundaries. What happened to that?"

"That was before..." Keanu trailed off. Before it all became a formula. Before the music stopped meaning anything except product and sales and charts. "Before everything got repetitive."

"Maybe she's not repetitive." Keanu exhaled slowly. Through the industrial windows, Stellarbrook was settling into evening, lights beginning to blink on across the hillside neighborhood. "Go for it, kid. Sometimes the best things come from the most unexpected places. You never know what might happen."

Something in Keanu's chest loosened. Just a fraction. "You sound like Callum."

"No, he sounds like me. I've been saying this shit for thirty years."

"Fair point."

"Besides," Lucas added, and Keanu could hear the smile in his voice, "what's the worst that could happen? You make some music. Maybe it's good, maybe it's not. But at least you tried something. Beats rotting on that balcony of yours."

"I don't rot. I contemplate."

"YOU ROT!" Callum shouted from somewhere in the background.

"I'm definitely hanging up now."

"Love you too, kid." Lucas was still laughing as the line went dead.

Keanu sat there in the dim light, phone resting against his thigh. The loft was quiet again, just him and the high ceilings and the exposed beams that had witnessed every breakdown and breakthrough of the past eight years.

Before he could chicken out, he pulled up Lu's contact and typed:

I'll do it.

The response came so fast Lu must have been waiting.

Good. Studio C, tomorrow, 2pm. Just you and Eden. You're self-producing.

Keanu stared at the screen. Wait, what?

Then another message filtered in.

You wanted creative control. You have it. Don't waste it.

"He hates me," Keanu said to the empty loft. "Lars actually hates me."
