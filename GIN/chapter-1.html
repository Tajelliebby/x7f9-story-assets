My arms are absolutely screaming.

The cardboard box shifts in my grip, corners digging into my forearms as I pause on the second-floor landing, chest heaving like I've just run a marathon instead of climbed two measly flights of stairs. The irony isn't lost on me—I spent four years bent over finance textbooks when I should've been hitting the campus gym.

"Just one more flight," I whisper to myself, adjusting my grip.

The older mover had looked so apologetic when he asked if I could handle just "these last few boxes," and honestly, how could I say no? I'm already paying them enough to move my entire life across the country. My phone buzzes against my hip. I manage to fish it out without dropping anything. A quick glance shows Cece's contact photo grinning back at me.

"Cece?" I answer, wedging the phone between my ear and shoulder.

"Juli! Please tell me you're not trying to move furniture by yourself."

"Just boxes," I pant, taking another step. "The elevator's broken, and—"

"What do you mean broken? Like, broken broken?"

I pause to catch my breath, leaning against the banister. The wrought iron is cool against my overheated palm. "There's a maintenance sign on it. Looks like it's been there for a while."

"Christ. Okay, well, don't kill yourself. I'm picking up Sebastian right now, but I can swing by later if you need—"

"I'm fine," I lie, even as my biceps start their second round of protests. "Just one more flight."

The truth is, I kind of need this moment alone. Moving out, starting over—it feels too big and fragile to share just yet, even with Cece. Like if I talk about it too much, the whole thing might dissolve.

"Alright, but call if you need anything. And Juli?"

"Yeah?"

"Welcome to freedom, babes."

Freedom. Yeah, that sounds about right. Even if freedom currently involves sweating through my favorite cotton tee and wondering if my arms will fall off before I reach the third floor.

"Call me when you're settled. I have news about Sebastian."

My heart does a little flip. "Good news?"

"The best. Love you, bye!"

The line goes dead just as I reach the third floor. I take a deep breath, tasting dust and the faint scent of someone's cooking—something with paprika and onions that makes my stomach growl. Real food, cooked by real people living their real lives. Soon I'll be one of them. I take another step, I'm so focused on not face-planting that I don't notice the box on top starting to slide until it's too late. I feel it happening in slow motion: the cardboard buckling, my books and picture frames sliding toward the edge.

"No, no, no," I gasp, trying to compensate by leaning back. But my foot catches on the step behind me, and suddenly I'm teetering backward, arms flailing, box tumbling—

The fall never comes.

Instead, there's a warm arm around my middle, steadying me, and a low voice right by my ear asking, "Are you okay?"

I'm pressed against something solid and reassuringly sturdy, breathing hard from adrenaline and embarrassment. The scent of clean laundry and something woody surrounds me.

"Oh God," I manage. "Thank you. I'm so sorry, I—"

"Hey, no worries." The voice is warm, tinged with genuine concern. "That looked like it was gonna hurt."

He helps me find my footing, and I turn to face my rescuer. The first thing I notice is his smile—ridiculously sunny, like he's actually happy to see me even though I nearly bowled him over. The second thing I notice is that he's built like someone who actually uses gym memberships, all broad shoulders and defined forearms that make my own noodle arms look even more pathetic.

"Let me guess," he says, eyeing the scattered contents of my box with amusement. "Third floor?"

I nod, suddenly very aware of how I must look—sweaty, disheveled, probably sporting some impressive pit stains. "How'd you know?"

"Lucky guess. Plus, you look like you're about to collapse." He's already gathering my scattered belongings. "Let me help with these," he says, repositioning the stack and lifting them out of my arms like they weigh nothing.

"You don't have to—"

"Trust me, I've been there." His grin is infectious. "Moving sucks enough."

"You're sure?" I ask. "I mean, you probably have better things to do than rescue clumsy neighbors... and thank you. Seriously. I thought I was about to redecorate the stairwell with my entire life."

He laughs—a rich, warm sound that echoes off the narrow walls. "Your life's got good taste in books," he says, glancing at the paperback romance novel in his hand. "But maybe we should get the rest of it upstairs before gravity tries again?"

"Yea," a sheepish smile tugs at my lips.

We start up the final flight, I find myself stealing glances at his profile. There's something familiar about him, but I can't place it.

"So you're new to the building?" he asks as we reach the third floor.

"New to everything, really."

"No kidding? What brought you to our little corner of chaos?"

"Work. And maybe a need for a completely fresh start." The words come out before I can stop them, more honest than I intended.

He doesn't push, just nods like he gets it. "Well, you picked a good spot. There's a corner store literally two minutes away—Sklep Pod Lipą. Old guy who runs it will have your coffee order memorized by next week. They have everything and won't bankrupt you."

"You are god-sent," I breathe, mentally adding this to my neighbourhood cheat sheet.

"I do try," he laughs. "And that jazz club across the street? Kamienica Różowa? Live music most nights. You should check it out sometime."

I glance down at the street below, noting the warm light spilling from windows across the way. "I might just do that. And what about the elevator situation? Please tell me it's getting fixed soon."

He winces. "Hate to break it to you, but it's been out for months. Pan Jacek—that's the building manager—kept promising they'd fix it 'next week' for about six months."

"So it's been broken for that long?"

"At least. Some of us have a running bet on whether it'll get fixed before the building falls down." He glances over at me with those crinkled eyes. "My money's on the building."

We reach the third floor, and I'm barely winded. Amazing. My apartment door stands open, and I can hear the movers inside, their voices echoing off the empty walls. "This is me," I say, gesturing toward 3D. "Thank you so much for—"

"No problem." He sets my boxes down just inside the threshold, those impressive muscles flexing under his t-shirt. "Welcome to Rezydencja Wisła," he says with a mock-formal bow. "Try not to let the broken elevator scare you away on day one."

"I'll do my best, and seriously, thank you."

"Anytime, neighbour."

Wait. Neighbors?

But he's already disappearing down the stairwell, leaving me staring after him with a dozen questions and the distinct feeling that moving here might have been the best decision I've made in months. The movers finish up twenty minutes later, leaving me alone with towers of cardboard boxes and the overwhelming reality of starting over. I walk through each room slowly, taking it all in—the spacious living area with its wall of windows, the gleaming kitchen that still smells faintly of new appliances, the balcony overlooking the tree-lined street below.

My balcony. My kitchen. My home.

The thought sends a thrill through me that's immediately dampened by the buzz of my phone. Unknown number. My stomach clenches as I read the message: Hey stranger, heard you moved. New city, new start?

I know that casual tone, that specific brand of manipulation disguised as friendly interest. My thumb hovers over the screen for a heartbeat before I delete the message and block the number.

"Not today Satan," I mutter, shoving the phone into my back pocket. "Not any day."

I survey my cardboard kingdom and make an executive decision: bedroom first, everything else later. I need somewhere to sleep tonight, and breaking this chaos into bite-sized pieces feels less like drowning. By the time I've wrestled my bed frame into submission and made it with fresh sheets I crash hard that night, face-planting into my pillows with the bone-deep satisfaction of someone who's finally doing something right. Seems sleep was exactly what the doctor ordered, I'm buzzing. By noon, I've created a small mountain of flattened cardboard boxes outside my door, just to make room to move around.

I'm wrestling with what might be the world's most complicated bookshelf assembly when I hear footsteps in the hallway then a hiss, "oh shit."

I look up to see the sandy blonde stumbling slightly, having walked straight into one of my cardboard boxes while absorbed in his phone call. He's wearing running shorts and a tank top, hair damp and I have to remind myself to breathe normally.

"I'm so sorry!" I scramble to my feet. "I should have stacked them better, or—"

"Hey, it's just cardboard." He grins, completely unbothered. "No harm done." His eyes drift past me into my apartment, landing on the bookshelf pieces scattered across my living room floor. "Assembly project?"

"Attempted assembly project. The instructions are in English and apparently I overestimated my furniture-building skills."

"Mind if I take a look? I'm pretty good with this stuff."

"Are you sure? I don't want to—"

"Trust me, it's no problem. English instructions are basically my superpower."

Relief floods through me. "Oh my god, yes please. I was about to throw the whole thing off my balcony."

He laughs, stepping over the cardboard debris. "Let's see what we're working with here."

That's when my phone rings. The sound cuts through the air like a blade, and when I see the number—different from yesterday, but I know, somehow I just know—my chest tightens. I answer without thinking.

"Juli, babe—"

I hang up immediately, fingers fumbling as I block this number too. How many phones does he have? How many times is he going to—

"Uh... you okay?"

I jump, having completely forgotten the knightly neighbour was there. He's standing in my doorway, genuine concern written across his features, and something about his expression—the way his brow furrows, the tilt of his head—triggers a memory I can't quite grasp.

"I'm fine," I say quickly, probably too quickly.

He studies my face for a moment, then nods toward his door. "Give me a second, yeah? I'll be right back."

To my surprise, he opens the door directly adjacent to mine—the corner apartment. The biggest one on the floor. And he just... leaves it open while he disappears inside. I can't help myself. I peek. Plants. So many plants. Hanging baskets, floor planters, what looks like a small herb garden by the windows. The afternoon light streams through, turning everything green and golden, and it's honestly the most peaceful thing I've seen all day. I settle back against my doorframe, the thoughts swirl in my head, but one keeps rising to the surface, clear and certain: I'm not going back. Not to him, not to the person I was when I was with him. Whatever it takes, however many numbers I have to block and I still have a bookshelf to build.

"Why are you outside?" I flinched. The neighbour had returned, concern shadowed those warm brown eyes. "Come on," he says gently, ushering me back into my apartment. "Sit. Drink this."

He guides me to my couch—one of the few pieces of furniture that's actually in its proper place—and hands me a thermos. I unscrew the top and my brow jumps. "Holy hell, this is strong." I bring it closer to my nose to take in that sweet, floral scent. "What is it?"

"Chamomile. Just picked it."

I blink at him. "You grow chamomile?"

He shrugs, settling cross-legged on my floor next to the bookshelf pieces. "I like plants."

There's something endearing about the understatement. From what I glimpsed of his apartment, "liking plants" is like saying the ocean is "sort of wet."

"Is it sweetened?" I ask, taking a cautious sip.

"Nope. You got honey? Sugar?"

I move toward my kitchen, then stop and literally facepalm. "I don't have anything yet. I'm an idiot."

"Honey or sugar?" he asks again, already getting to his feet.

"Honey, if you don't mind."

"No problem." He heads toward his door. "And we should definitely hit the supermarket soon. You've got nothing."

He returns with an entire bottle of honey, sets it on my kitchen island like it's the most natural thing in the world, then gets back to work on my bookshelf. It bizarrely felt right.
