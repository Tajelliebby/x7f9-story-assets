I roll over as sun rays slip through my curtains and dance across my face. Just as I'm sitting up, my phone blares to life, scaring the absolute shit out of me. But when I see it's just my alarm, I'm grinning like an idiot.

"Good morning, new life," I mumble, hurrying out of bed.

I'm brushing my teeth when my hand slows down mid-scrub. Wait a minute.

I never got his name. Hot neighbour guy. Furniture-assembling, tea-making, literally-caught-me-falling guy, and I have no idea what to call him. How do you see someone twice, have them basically save your life, and still not know their name?

"Meh," I spit into the sink. "We live on the same floor. I can ask eventually."

Hours later, I have to give it to Mom—her foresight to move me weeks ahead of my new job was genius. I step back until my shoulders hit the front door, taking in the full sweep of my open-concept living space. The afternoon light filters through gauzy curtains, catching the warm sandy wood of my furniture and making the cream walls glow.

God, I did such a good job. I'm actually in love with this place. Which means Sebastian can finally come home.

The thought sends a thrill through me. My baby has been staying with Cece since I couldn't handle both the move and a cat, but now that everything's set up his feline majesty can reclaim his kingdom. And not only is Cece bringing him, but Zofia and Sylwia are coming too. My first real housewarming party. I'm practically bouncing as I arrange mismatched plates and cups on the counter, the spring rain pattering softly against my windows.

A sharp knock makes me squeak and drop a fork. I hurry to the door, "Juli!" She launches herself at me, pure energy, her dark curls wild from the humidity and we hug like we haven't seen each other in decades instead of weeks.

A loud, indignant meow separates us.

"Oh, don't be so dramatic," Cece scolds, crouching down to unlatch the carrier door. "You're home now, you spoiled little prince."

Sebastian emerges with the dignity of royalty inspecting new territory. His black coat gleams under my entryway lighting, those impossible blue eyes taking in every corner as he struts past us like he owns the place. Which, let's be honest, he absolutely does now.

"Where's all his stuff?" I ask, watching him disappear into the living room to begin his investigation.

"Oh!" Cece snaps her fingers. "Kaje's bringing it up. I called him to help me dismantle everything this morning—that cat tower was a nightmare to take apart."

Heat creeps up my neck. "Kajetan's here?"

"Ew, please don't call him that. Literally no one uses his full name except Mom when she's pissed." Cece makes a gagging face just as footsteps echo in the hallway.

"What's wrong with my name, Cecylia?"

I turn to see Kaje filling my doorway, arms loaded with boxes, that familiar sunny grin on his face. My brain does a little stutter-step as pieces click into place—my helpful neighbor, Cece's brother, the scrawny kid I vaguely remember from years ago.

"It's a perfectly good name," he continues, mock-wounded. "You're just lazy."

"Where do you want these?" he asks me, shifting the boxes in his arms.

I freeze, mouth opening and closing like a fish. Cece rolls her eyes and points toward the living room. "Corner by the window, genius. That's where we set up his whole setup at home."

Kaje nods and heads inside, and I watch those impressive biceps flex as he carries what looks like half of Sebastian's worldly possessions. How is it that every time I see this man, he's lifting something heavy?

"You've done wonders with this place," he says, setting the boxes down and looking around. "Last time I was here it was just boxes and chaos."

"Thanks," I manage, but mentally I'm doing Olympic-level gymnastics. My cute, helpful neighbor is Cece's brother.

Where the hell did that scrawny kid go?

"You've changed so much," I blurt out, then immediately want to crawl under my coffee table.

He ruffles his bleached hair, dimples deepening. "Got into sports in college. Plus this building has a gym in the basement, so why not make use of it, right?" The smile is the same though—pure sunshine, just like I remember. Cece smacks his arm, making him grab at it dramatically. "Ow! What was that for?"

"You never introduced yourself to Juli, did you?" She holds my face between her hands like she's presenting evidence. "Older brother, Juliana didn't realize it was you!"

"Oh?" His eyes widen. "Oh! Oh shit, I'm sorry. I never meant to lie to you or anything—"

"It's fine," I assure him quickly, though my cheeks are burning. "I guess that's why I felt so comfortable around my mysterious helpful neighbour."

"Yea?" He grins, all dimples and mischief. "Pretty cool coincidence. Same building, same floor. What are the odds?"

"Yeah..." I trail off, then spin toward Cece. "Wait, why didn't you tell me Kaje lived here?"

She looks genuinely affronted. "You didn't tell me where you were moving! How would I have known?"

I deflate. "Well, yeah..." The truth is, I kept my moving plans close to my chest, afraid a certain someone would catch wind of them. But still. "Let's get Sebby's kingdom set up before the others arrive," I say, shaking my head. "They'll be here soon."

We work together in comfortable chaos—Kaje reassembling the elaborate cat tower while Cece and I arrange feeding stations and toy baskets. The April air drifts through my cracked balcony door, carrying the scent of rain and new leaves. What's surreal is watching Sebastian hover around Kaje like they're old friends. My antisocial prince, who usually hides from anyone with a Y chromosome, is supervising Kaje's every move, occasionally head-butting his leg or purring when those large hands scratch behind his ears.

I feel weirdly betrayed. I'm right here, Sebastian. I'm the one who missed you.

"He remembers you," I say, digging through my memory. "You two met before, right?"

"Yeah, few times over the years when I'd visit home." Kaje glances up from tightening a bolt on the cat tower. "He was always cooler than me though."

"Still is," Cece adds helpfully.

By the time we finish, Sebastian has claimed his throne on the top perch, blue eyes surveying his domain with satisfaction. The rain has stopped, and golden evening light streams through the windows.

Cece and Kaje head out to grab food for the party, leaving me alone with my newly-crowned feline overlord. I pour myself a glass of wine and wait for the others, watching Sebastian test out each level of his tower with the seriousness of a safety inspector.

At exactly seven, my door swung open. "The chicken is here!" Cece announces, bursting back through my door.

"So are drinks!" Sylwia adds, producing a bottle of Żubrówka that makes my mouth water.

"My saviors," I declare dramatically, and suddenly my apartment is alive with laughter and the clink of mismatched glasses.

My apartment was filled with the heavenly scent of grilled chicken, laughter and chatter and the kind of warmth that makes everything feel perfect.

"To Juli!" Cece raises her glass once everyone has drinks. "For being brave enough to start over!"

"To new beginnings!" Zofia adds.

"To finally having a place where we can drink without your parents judging us," Sylwia grins.

We clink glasses, and the praise makes my cheeks warm. "Mine first!" I reach for the bag Cece suddenly thrusts at me. Inside is the most gorgeous tea set I've ever seen—delicate porcelain with hand-painted cherry blossoms, accompanied by an assortment of tea leaves that smell like heaven.

"Cece, this is beautiful," I breathe, running my fingers over the smooth ceramic.

"Of course it is. I know you better than you know yourself."

Zofia and Sylwia push their gifts toward me in perfect synchronization, because of course they do. Vintage movie posters from Zofia—the kind that make you look cultured and mysterious. From Sylwia, a miniature herb garden that smells like possibility.

"For your kitchen," she grins. "Now you have no excuse not to cook."

"Great, now I just need to learn how."

The evening dissolves into the warm blur of good food, strong drinks, and the kind of laughter that makes your cheeks ache. We sprawl across my cream couch and floor cushions, Sebastian claiming his throne atop his new cat tower, surveying his subjects with regal approval.

It's Zofia who brings up the elephant in the room, because of course it's Zofia.

"So," she says, swirling her drink with practiced nonchalance, "any word from He-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named?"

"He called," I admit, my stomach clenching at the memory. "Yesterday. Blocked him again."

Sylwia pushes her stylish glasses up her nose. "How many numbers is that now?"

"Three? Four? I've lost count."

"At least he hasn't shown his ugly face in person," Cece mutters around a chicken wing.

Zofia snorts into her drink. "After that ass-whooping your five-foot-nothing friend delivered in front of the entire campus? I'd stay hidden too."

"Hey!" Cece springs to her feet, waving her wing at Zofia. "I'm five-foot-two, get it right!"

We dissolve into laughter, and for a while, my ex is forgotten in the warm glow of friendship and too much wine.

"But seriously though," Sylwia says once we've calmed down, "congratulations on the job. An internship that turned into full employment? That's amazing."

"Thanks to Z for letting me know about it when she did." I raise my glass toward Zofia, who works for the same financial firm I'll be starting at next week.

"Of course. Love to have a friend around." Zofia grins. "Plus you're actually competent, which is more than I can say for some of our recent hires."

"But wait," Sylwia frowns slightly, "are you going to leave Sebastian alone all day? He's never really been alone before, right?"

The question hits me like a bucket of cold water. She's right—at my parents' house, there was always someone around. Mom works from home, Dad's retired. Sebastian's never been truly alone for eight hours at a time.

"I..." I start, then stop, panic rising in my chest. What kind of pet owner am I if I didn't think this through?

"I could watch him," Kaje offers quietly. "I work from home most days anyway, and he doesn't seem to dislike me."

I stare at him. "Are you sure? That's a lot to ask—"

"It's not asking if I'm offering." His smile is gentle. "Plus look at him."

We all turn to see Sebastian sprawled belly-up in his new cubby, completely relaxed and clearly feeling at home. "Okay," I say, relief flooding through me. "Thank you. Seriously."

Around ten, Kaje stands and stretches. "I should probably walk you ladies to your cars. It's getting late."

There's a chorus of protests, but he's already gathering empty bottles and being generally helpful in that way of his. Eventually, everyone gathers their things and heads toward the door. I close it behind them, then lean against it for a moment, taking in the quiet of my apartment. Sebastian has migrated to my couch, claiming the corner like it was made for him.

Then, in a burst of wine-fueled courage, I yank the door open and rush into the hallway. "Kaje!"

He turns, midway down the stairs, looking mildly alarmed. "Juli? Everything okay?"

"I just—" I grip the doorframe, suddenly aware I'm in my socks and probably look ridiculous. "Thank you. For everything. The past couple days, today, offering to watch Sebastian... just, thank you."

His expression shifts from alarm to something warm and bright, dimples appearing as his smile spreads. "Anytime."
