Juli

Maybe I shouldn’t have taken the weekend so lightly, because Monday was pure hell. How was week two somehow worse than week one? The first sign of doom, I was late. My phone sat silent on the nightstand, its screen mocking me with the time 7:47 AM. If it wasn’t for Sebastian’s internal tummy clock and his insistent meowing directly into my ear, I’d still be unconscious.

“Shit, shit, shit.” I scrambled out of bed, nearly tripping over my own feet. Sebastian watched from his perch on my pillow, tail twitching with what I swear was amusement.

I rushed around the apartment like a madwoman. Hair in the world’s messiest bun, mascara applied while hopping into my pencil skirt, blazer thrown over one arm while I checked my handbag. Phone, wallet, metro card—

“Where’s my ID?” I muttered, dumping the contents of my bag onto the kitchen counter. Lipstick, receipts, loose mints, but no badge. A knock echoed through the apartment. I glanced at the peephole to see Kajetan standing there, holding up something small and rectangular. I yanked the door open.

“I don’t think this belongs to me.” He was smiling, but his brows were slightly furrowed, concerned. “Rough start?”

“Ugh, yes. You’re a lifesaver.” I snatched my badge from his fingers, running back to the counter to repack my bag. “I woke up late and I’m sorry—Sebastian isn’t even in his carrier for you yet.”

“No biggie.” Before I could protest, Kaje stepped past me and scooped up Sebastian, who immediately went limp in his arms like the absolute traitor he was. “You’re in a hurry. I’ll lock up for you.” He lifted Sebastian’s paw and made him wave at me. “Sebastian, say bye to your mommy.”

Despite my panic, I couldn’t help but smile. I leaned over to kiss Sebastian’s fuzzy head, and he gave me one of those slow, contented blinks. It wasn’t until I straightened that I realized how close that had brought me to Kaje. Close enough to catch the faint scent of his shower gel, close enough to see the flecks of gold in his eyes. Dangerously domestic, indeed.

“Thanks,” I said quickly, pressing my house key into his palm and trying to ignore the brief contact of our fingers. I did a final sweep of the apartment, grabbed my travel mug, and headed for the stairs. Thanks to missing my usual metro, I arrived at Białek & Co. fifteen minutes late, slightly out of breath, and definitely not ready for Marek’s disapproving stare.

“Punctuality, Kowalczyk,” he said without looking up from his monitor when I slid into my desk at 8:43. “Is a basic professional expectation.”

“Yes, sir. Sorry, sir.”

For the rest of the morning, Marek was on my ass about everything. A misplaced decimal in a projection. A formatting issue in my report. The way I organized my files. I just wanted a break, but adulting apparently sucked like this.

By lunch, I felt like I’d been put through a meat grinder. I practically stumbled out of the office, desperate for fresh air and friendly faces. That’s when I spotted Tomek near the elevators. I was ready to dash in the opposite direction when I noticed a girl who looked like she was being cornered. Petite, pastel pink sweater, deer-in-headlights expression. Not one to ignore someone in a situation similar to my own, I channeled my inner Cecylia and strode over with purpose.

“There you are!” I called to the girl. “We’re going to be late for our lunch reservation.”

“Oh! Yes, of course,” she said quickly, catching on.

Tomek turned, his smile lifting higher when he saw me. “Juli. I didn’t realize you two knew each other.”

“Since university,” I lied smoothly. “Excuse us, we’re meeting Zofia and Sylwia.”

“I was just inviting…” he paused, realizing he didn’t know her name.

“Ewa,” she supplied quietly.

“…Ewa to join some of us for coffee later.”

I linked my arm through hers. “She’ll be there if her schedule allows. Now if you’ll excuse us.”

Once the elevator doors closed, Ewa let out a shaky breath. “Dziękuję. That was… intense.”

“Tomasz Zieliński,” I explained. “Office predator. Steer clear.”

“I’m Ewa Malinowska,” she offered her hand. “Marketing department.”

“Juli Kowalczyk. Financial analysis.” I shook her hand. “And you’re brave for choosing marketing.”

She laughed softly. “I know who you are. You were pointed out at orientation as ‘the one who impressed Marek,’ which apparently is like spotting a unicorn.”

I snorted. “Well this unicorn has enough work for three people.”

When we reached the café across the street, I spotted the old man from last week. He looked up when we entered, making direct eye contact with me. He offered a polite smile and nod, which I returned. Zofia and Sylwia had already claimed our usual table.

“Ladies, this is Ewa from Marketing,” I announced as we sat. “I rescued her from Tomek’s clutches, so be nice.”

“You saved her from Tomek the Terrible?” Zofia deadpanned, sliding over to make room. “Welcome to our support group for women who’ve had to interact with him.”

“Any enemy of Tomek’s is a friend of ours,” Sylwia added with a warm smile.

We were halfway through our sandwiches when I felt it—that prickly sensation of being watched. I glanced around casually and spotted an older man at a corner table, his eyes fixed directly on me. When our gazes met, but he didn’t look away. Just kept staring. A chill ran down my spine despite the warm café air. I rubbed my arms, suddenly feeling too exposed despite my blazer and blouse.

“You okay?” Zofia asked, following my gaze.

“Yeah, just…” I forced a smile. “I think I’m gonna head back early. This morning was brutal.”

The afternoon was worse. Marek seemed determined to make up for every second of my tardiness by piling on more work. By the time he finally released me at 6 PM, I felt like I’d been run over by a freight train. I sighed, gathered my things, and trudged home.

When I finally reached my apartment building, it took me rummaging through my bag outside my apartment door to remember I didn’t have my key. I knocked on Kaje’s door. Silence. I knocked again, harder this time, and heard shuffling from inside. The door opened to show a side of him I’d never seen before—hair sticking up in six different directions, covered in black cat fur, and clearly fresh from sleep. A crease from a pillow ran down his left cheek.

I giggled, feeling some of my stress evaporate. Behind him, I could see the makeshift nest on his living room floor—blankets and pillows scattered everywhere, with Sebastian stretching in the center like the king of his domain.

“Hey,” his sleep heavy voice caressed my ears as worried eyes scanned my face. “You okay?”

“Yeah, just a little stressed.” Understatement of the decade.

He stepped aside, gesturing me in. “Come on. Sit down.”

“I should really just grab Sebastian and—”

“Juli. Sit.” His voice was gentle but firm. “I’ll cook something up,” already shuffling toward the kitchen.

“You don’t need to—”

“I’m offering.” He glanced back at me. “Why don’t you snuggle up beside Sebastian? You’ll feel better.”

I couldn’t deny that logic. I slipped off my blazer, kicked off my heels, and curled up on the blanket beside my cat. Sebastian immediately rolled over to press against my side, purring like a tiny motor. The makeshift nest was ridiculously comfortable, surrounded by the gentle rustle of leaves and the warm, earthy smell of his apartment.

Before I knew it, I’d drifted off.

I don’t know how long I’d been asleep when Kaje gently shook my shoulder. “Hey, sleeping beauty. Dinner’s ready.”

The savory smell hit me first—rich, comforting, like home. The warmth of the room, the golden light filtering through his plants, the soft rumble of Sebastian’s purr against my ribs. I must have been asleep for hours.

“Halupki?” I questioned, recognizing the stuffed cabbage smell. “I can’t even boil water and you’re out here making Halupki from scratch?”

He shrugged, looking almost embarrassed. “Learned a few things in my college days.”

That didn’t answer anything, but I wasn’t complaining. Halupki and mashed potatoes, actual comfort food when I needed it most. After we ate, Kaje let me pack away the leftovers while he washed dishes, and I’d never felt more like a leech. Every time I tried to help, he’d gently redirect me away from the sink.

“Key?” I asked when we finished.

He handed it over and I took Sebastian home for his dinner, then returned to find Kaje plating the leftovers into two containers, another small portion as I approached the kitchen island.

“Take these.” He handed me one of the containers, and I felt my throat tighten unexpectedly. I felt like I could cry, and honestly, maybe I looked like I would too, because Kaje quickly came around the island. “What’s wrong? You feeling pain somewhere?”

I shook my head quickly. “No, I just… thank you. Really.”

His expression softened, and he reached out to ruffle my hair. “Get some rest, okay? Tomorrow will be better.”

I nodded, clutching the container of leftovers. “Triple-checking my alarm tonight.”

“Good plan.”

Tuesday started much better. My alarm actually worked, Sebastian got his breakfast on time, and I made it to the metro without breaking into a sprint. The transition from home to work went smoothly—no panicked searches for missing items, no judgmental stares from Marek. The day wasn’t half bad until the ten o’clock meeting. Then it felt like someone had shot me in the foot.

“Given the complexity of the Komorowski merger,” Marek announced to our gathered team, “management has decided to bring in additional expertise.” That’s when Tomek walked in, looking like he owned the place in his crisp navy suit. “Tomasz Zieliński from Business Development will be assisting with strategy,” Marek continued.

Tomek’s eyes found mine across the conference table. “I’m looking forward to working with you all,” he said smoothly, his gaze lingering. “Especially our new talent.”

Ew.

I spent the rest of the meeting volunteering for every task that would keep me as far from Tomek’s oversight as possible. I don’t care if it meant having enough work to last until Christmas. By lunch, I was buried so deep in spreadsheets I missed the girls’ usual café run. My stomach was practically eating itself when I finally surfaced at half past one, so I grabbed my wallet and headed downstairs alone.

The café was quieter, I ordered a sandwich and found a small table by the window, scrolling through my phone while I ate. That familiar prickly feeling crept up my spine again. I glanced up to see the older man approaching my table, coffee cup in hand and that same polite smile on his face. “Excuse me, miss. May I.”

I don’t have to look around, the café was half empty. “Of course,” I said anyway, because what else could I say?

He settled into the chair across from me, taking a few sips from his cup. “Busy day at the office?”

“Always is.” I kept my voice light.

“I can imagine. This district— it’s gotten so hectic over the years. Used to be you could walk down the street without dodging suits every five meters.” He chuckled, shaking his head. “Progress, I suppose. You work at Białek & Co, don’t you? Saw you coming out yesterday.”

My chewing slowed. “Yeah.”

His eyes crinkled at the corners. “My daughter, she’s about your age. Always tells me I don’t understand what young people do for work these days.”

Harmless enough. So why did my gut feel like it was twisted in knots?

“The weather’s been lovely, hasn’t it?” he continued. “Perfect for walking. I like to take evening strolls around the neighbourhood—helps clear the head after a long day.”

“That’s nice,” I managed, “it’s good that you get some exercise.”

“Exactly as my daughter says,” he chuckled sipping the last of his coffee. “Well, I won’t keep you from your lunch.” He stood, coffee barely touched. “Have a lovely afternoon, miss.”

He walked away, leaving me sitting there with my heart hammering against my ribs for absolutely no logical reason. The conversation had been completely innocent—weather, work, walking. So why did I feel like I needed a shower?

I wrapped up the rest of my sandwich and practically fled back to the office, checking over my shoulder twice before I made it to the elevator. The afternoon crawled by in a haze of numbers and spreadsheets. Every time someone walked past my desk, I looked up, half expecting to see either Tomek or that old man. Neither appeared, but the anxiety sat in my chest like a stone anyway.

By five o’clock, I was desperate to get out of there. I gathered my things quickly, hoping to make a clean escape before anyone could corner me with more work.

“Juli! There you are.”

Damn it so close.

That cologne hit me before his voice did. Tomek appeared beside me at the elevators, standing just close enough that I had to resist the urge to step back. “Leaving already?” he asked, like five-fifteen was somehow early.

“It’s past five,” I pointed out, jabbing the elevator button harder than necessary.

“The Komorowski presentation is next week. We should coordinate our sections.”

“I’ll email you my slides tomorrow.” Come on, elevator. Move.

“I prefer to discuss these things in person. Gets the creative juices flowing, you know?” He leaned against the wall, blocking my view of the floor indicator. “Perhaps tomorrow, lunch?”

The elevator dinged finally and I stepped in quickly. “Sorry, I’ll catch you in the morning meeting.”

His smile tightened around the edges. “Count on it.”

As the doors slid shut on his face, I let out a breath I didn’t realize I’d been holding. The reflection in the elevator’s polished walls showed a woman who looked frazzled and jumpy, nothing like the composed professional I was supposed to be. Outside, the evening air felt like freedom. But even as I hurried toward the metro station, that feeling of being watched clung to me like smoke.
