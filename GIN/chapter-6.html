Juli

First month in my new apartment, two and a half weeks into my new job, this was much more of a rollercoaster than I’d expected. The April rain drummed against my windows with an insistence that matched my growing unease, each droplet a reminder that Polish spring weather was as unpredictable as everything else in my life lately. From my kitchen window, I could see the neon glow of Nowogródek’s business district bleeding through the gray morning.

Should I just go back home?

No, no, Juli. You’re a big girl. You can’t run home crying over every little detail. Besides, he who shall not be named was probably still back in Starewicz, and he was the last person I wanted to see. Ever. But even when I wasn’t seeing him, he was still a pain in my ass. Two calls this past week, as if I didn’t have enough on my plate with Marek and Tomek breathing down my neck at Białek & Co. I already needed a vacation, but I wasn’t eligible yet. When was the next public holiday anyway? May Day?

The morning routine felt heavier than usual as I stood in my bathroom, glaring at my reflection while attempting to tame a stubborn strand of hair that refused to cooperate. The fluorescent light above the mirror cast harsh shadows under my eyes. When had I started looking so tired?

“This is as good as it’s going to get,” I muttered, finally surrendering to the hair gods. The city beyond my window was waking up too, the distant hum of traffic on Ulica Szklana growing louder. “What do you think, Sebby? Should I grab a pączek on the way to work?” I crouched to scratch behind his ears, earning a rumbling purr. “Maybe some chocolate cake?”

Sebastian fixed me with one of his judgment looks. The kind that said really, human?

“Yeah, maybe not cake yet. Being responsible sucks,” I conceded, watching through the window as the early morning joggers braved the drizzle. “Well, it’s Friday. Maybe I could get some on the weekend.”

As I got dressed, I found myself reaching for pants instead of the skirt I’d laid out the night before. It felt like I was being stared at more than usual lately, and while in another time, another place, maybe I would’ve felt flattered, all I felt now was spooked. I opted for dark trousers with my predetermined blouse and blazer. Professional but covered. Safe.

At the appointed time, I handed Sebastian over to Kaje at his door. “Does Sebastian have a harness?” he asked, accepting my cat’s carrier with those careful hands that I’d noticed were always gentle with fragile things.

“He does. Why?” I went back to fetch it, curious about what architectural adventure my cat was about to embark on.

“I might need to go on site later, so between the harness and the carrier, I’ll take him along.” He accepted the small harness, examining the buckles with the same attention he probably gave his blueprints. There was something endearing about watching him figure out the mechanics of cat accessories.

I booped Sebastian’s pink nose through the carrier mesh, my heart doing something stupid at how domestic this felt. “Behave for your favorite uncle, okay, Your Highness?”

Sebastian meowed in response, which I chose to interpret as agreement rather than the indignant protest it probably was. Kaje’s laugh followed me down the hallway, warm and rich like good coffee. “We’ll be fine. Have a good day at work.”

I won’t say he jinxed me, but man, I wish I’d never clocked in.

The commute to Srebrocentrum felt longer than usual, the metro car packed with the usual suspects—business suits clutching coffee cups like lifelines, students with their faces buried in textbooks, tourists consulting maps with bewildered expressions. The glass towers of the business district loomed ahead as we emerged from Stacja Centrum Metro, their surfaces reflecting the gray sky like mirrors of my mood.

Białek Tower stretched thirty-five floors into the cloudy sky, its lobby a study in intimidating minimalism. All marble and steel, with security guards who looked like they could bench press a small car. The elevator ride to the thirty-second floor felt like ascending into corporate purgatory. The conference room had felt suffocating, its floor-to-ceiling windows offering a view of the city that should have been inspiring but instead felt like a reminder of how small I was in this vast urban machine.

Marek had spent the entire afternoon dismantling my analysis in front of the entire team, his thick eyebrows knitting together with each slide as if my projections had personally offended his ancestors. “This projection is too conservative, Kowalczyk. The client expects growth, not stagnation.”

Tomek had sat across from me, his pale eyes tracking my every reaction to Marek’s criticism with something that looked suspiciously like satisfaction. “Perhaps,” Tomek had interjected with that oily smile of his, “we should consider a more… collaborative approach to the next presentation.”

The way he’d said ‘collaborative’ made my skin crawl.

Friday kicked my ass so thoroughly I was genuinely considering whether it was too late to crawl back to my mother. No, no, Juli. You’re a big girl. Big girls can do this… Big girls also wished they had cake and beer, but apparently, we couldn’t have nice things. Thank God we got off at four on Fridays at least.

The journey home felt like swimming upstream against the tide of weekend commuters. By the time I reached our building in Osiedle Lipowe Wzgórza, the afternoon sun was struggling to break through the lingering clouds. As I climbed the stairs, cursing management to please fix the apartment elevator, I spotted Kaje by his door. Sebastian was on his harness, looking regal as ever despite the tiny leather straps, while Kaje held a pink box in his other hand. He was saying something to Sebastian that I couldn’t quite catch, but my cat meowed and turned to look at me, which made Kaje glance up.

His face lit up with that sunny smile that should come with a warning label about potential cardiac events. “You’re back! Welcome home.”

Ugh, he’s so bright. “Good to be home,” I managed, feeling heat creep up my neck.

He gestured toward his apartment. “Come on in. I got something that might improve your day.”

Inside his apartment, Kaje set the pink box on his kitchen counter while Sebastian immediately made himself comfortable on the couch, still wearing his harness like it was designer clothing.

“Sebastian and I went to a work site today,” Kaje explained, untying the string on the box with careful fingers. “On the way back, I stopped by Piekarnia Wiśniowa— that bakery near our building. Thought you might like this.”

He opened the box to reveal a perfect slice of chocolate tort, its glossy ganache catching the afternoon light streaming through his kitchen window. My heart did something stupid in my chest. “Can you read minds, or did Sebastian snitch on me?”

Kaje’s laugh was sudden and bright, filling every corner of his kitchen like sunshine. “What?”

“I was literally telling him this morning that I was craving chocolate cake.” I pointed an accusatory finger at my traitorous cat, who was now grooming his paw with studied innocence. “Did you tell him, Sebby?”

Sebastian simply blinked at me.

“Lucky guess,” Kaje said, still grinning as he handed me a fork. The brief contact of our fingers sent sparks up my arm. “But your face right now is worth whatever Sebastian may or may not have told me.”

I closed my eyes on the first bite, letting out an involuntary sound of appreciation that made me immediately embarrassed. The cake was perfect. Rich and moist without being too sweet, with that deep chocolate flavor.

“Better?” Kaje asked, leaning against the counter with his arms crossed, the motion pulling his shirt tight across his chest in a way that was definitely not helping my concentration.

“So much better. This day definitely needed chocolate.” I took another bite, savoring not just the taste but the thoughtfulness behind it. “Wait, you said work site. How was Sebastian with your crew?”

“Got more attention than a pluszak at a children’s birthday party,” Kaje said fondly, easing off the counter to cross his apartment to his couch. “Though he did hiss at my boss,” he scratched behind Sebastian’s ears.

“Sebastian!” I gasped in mock horror. “Terrible manners.”

“Actually, I think he’s an excellent judge of character.” Kaje winked, and I had to grip the fork tighter to keep from doing something embarrassing. Then his expression shifted slightly, becoming more serious. “Listen, I have to leave for a bit. Work thing.”

“Oh.” The drop in my stomach was immediate and embarrassing. “Everything okay?”

Thankfully, he didn’t seem to notice. “I need to stop a particularly eccentric client from making a design disaster. Might take a day or two, maybe more depending on how stubborn they’re being. I’ve asked Cece to stay over while I’m away.”

“You don’t have to—”

He looked up at me. “Think of it as a week-long girls’ night.”

Before I could protest that I didn’t need a babysitter my phone rang. Cece’s name flashed on the screen. “Speak of the devil,” I muttered, answering. “Hey, Cece.”

“Juli!” Her enthusiasm practically vibrated through the phone. “Girl, I’m so excited we get to spend a few days together! I’ve already dismantled part of my setup and I’m carrying it over now. I’ll get the rest tomorrow.”

I laughed despite myself. “Okay, but—”

“Hold up,” Cece interrupted. “You don’t sound great. What’s wrong?”

Trust Cece to cut straight to the heart of things. “Work’s been kicking my ass.”

“Then it’s perfect timing for a girls’ weekend. I’ll be there in like twenty minutes, and we’re going out. Nails, face masks, the whole shebang.”

“Oh no, I can’t do that to your brother. He’s been watching Sebastian all day—”

“It’s fine,” Kaje called out, having obviously heard every word. “I need to pack anyway.”

“See?” Cece’s voice was smug. “No excuses. We’re having fun whether you like it or not.”

When the call ended, I turned to Kaje. “I’ll help you pack until she gets here.”

“You don’t have to.”

I smiled, using his own words against him. “I know, but I’m offering.”

He conceded with that laugh that could make daisies bloom in winter. Seriously, it needed to come with a warning label about potential cardiac events. We moved to his bedroom, and I tried very hard not to think about how intimate this felt—helping him fold clothes, organizing his belongings.

“Where exactly are you going?” I asked after a few minutes, my curiosity finally winning out over my attempts to be casual.

“Gdańsk. There’s this cultural center project—they want a public garden.” He pulled jeans from a drawer, the denim soft and worn. “Nothing exciting, but the client is… particular.”

“Particular how?”

“The kind who changes their mind every other day and expects miracles.” His expression was fond but exasperated. “Last month they wanted modern minimalist. Now they’re leaning toward English cottage garden.”

“In the middle of a cultural center?”

“Exactly. Hence the emergency trip before they decide they want a tropical rainforest theme next.”

I rolled his socks the way my sisters had taught me. “Sounds like a nightmare client.”

“The worst kind. But the project’s too good to walk away from.” He watched me work with what looked like fascination. “You’re really good at this.”

“Three sisters, remember?” I hummed, not looking up from the perfectly paired socks.

“Three sisters? I only know about Joasia.”

“The others are back in Starewicze. Ania’s married with twins, and Magda’s finishing her master’s program.” My hands stilled for a moment as homesickness washed over me. “They think I’m crazy for moving to Warsaw.”

“Are you? Crazy, I mean.”

I considered this, folding a sweater. The question felt heavier than it should. “Maybe. But sometimes crazy is exactly what you need.”

Right as we finished organizing his bag, a knock echoed through the apartment. “Halo! It’s your favorite person!”

“That’s debatable,” Kaje called back, but he was smiling as he went to let his sister in.

I stayed in the bedroom, listening to the siblings bicker good-naturedly in the living room. Something twisted in my chest, a mix of anticipation and loss. I was going to miss him, even if it was just for a few days.

Get a grip, Juli. He’s going to Gdańsk, not Mars.

“Juli!” Cece appeared in the doorway, arms full of what looked like half her apartment, dark curls bouncing with infectious energy. “Ready for some sister time?”

“More than ready,” I said, meaning it.

“Put those down first,” Kaje nudged her gently.

She dumped her stuff in his guest room with characteristic chaos, then grabbed my hand, tugging me toward the door. “We’ll see you later,” she called to her brother.

“Have fun, you two,” Kaje called as Cece dragged me out into the hallway.

“Where exactly are we going?” I asked as we headed for the stairs, the evening air carrying the scent of linden blossoms and distant rain.

“Everywhere,” Cece said with a grin that was pure mischief. “Starting with getting you out of those work clothes and into something that says ‘I’m young and fabulous and Friday night is mine.’”

“Alright,” I said, letting her enthusiasm pull me along. “But I’m warning you, I’m a lightweight when it comes to wine.”

“Perfect,” Cece laughed. “This is going to be so much fun.”
